TOPIC:
Over-preparing the ask beats winging it in pitch meetings

HOOK:
The Best Founders Don't Improvise Their Ask

INTRO:
"I'll just see how the conversation goes" sounds relaxed. Point 2 is why it costs you.

POINT 1:
Winging a pitch feels natural in the moment and vague in the recording, if anyone's taking notes.

POINT 2:
The founders who close well have usually rehearsed the exact ask out loud, more than once, before the room.

POINT 3:
A scripted ask isn't stiff - it's the difference between "let me know what you think" and a clear yes or no.

CAPTION:
"I don't like sounding rehearsed" is a sentence said by a lot of founders right before a vague, forgettable pitch.

Winging it feels authentic in the moment. In practice, it usually means the ask gets softer, the numbers get fuzzier, and the meeting ends with "let's stay in touch" instead of a decision.

The founders who close well aren't more charismatic. They've said the exact ask out loud enough times that it comes out clean under pressure.

Preparation isn't the opposite of authenticity. It's what makes the authentic version land.
