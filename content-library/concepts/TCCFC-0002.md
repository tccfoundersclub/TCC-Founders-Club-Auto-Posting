TOPIC:
Hiring a friend into a senior role changes the friendship permanently

HOOK:
Hiring Your Friend Changes the Friendship, Not Just the Org Chart

INTRO:
It feels like the safest hire you'll ever make. Point 3 is what it actually risks.

POINT 1:
A friend feels like a safe hire because you already trust them. Trust isn't the same as fit for the role.

POINT 2:
Giving feedback to an employee is hard. Giving hard feedback to a friend who's now your employee is harder, and most founders quietly avoid it.

POINT 3:
When it doesn't work out, you don't just lose an employee. You usually lose the friendship too, because neither side prepared for that version of the relationship.

CAPTION:
Hiring your friend feels like the safest decision on the list. It's actually one of the riskiest, and almost nobody warns you why.

It's not about competence. It's about what happens the first time they need real, direct feedback and you're now the boss instead of the friend. Most founders flinch, avoid the conversation, and let performance issues slide long after they would have addressed them in anyone else.

By the time it becomes unavoidable, you're not just managing an underperformer. You're managing the possible end of a friendship.

If you're about to hire a friend, have the "what happens if this doesn't work" conversation before the offer, not after.
