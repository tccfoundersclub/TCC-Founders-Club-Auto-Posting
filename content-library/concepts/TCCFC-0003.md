TOPIC:
Asking every mentor to review your deck burns goodwill fast

HOOK:
Asking Every Mentor to "Just Take a Look at My Deck" Is Burning You Out With Them

INTRO:
It feels like free due diligence. Point 2 is what it actually signals.

POINT 1:
Sending your deck to five mentors "for a quick look" spreads a lot of asking around a small amount of trust.

POINT 2:
A vague "what do you think?" is more work for them than a specific question - and specific questions get better answers anyway.

POINT 3:
The founders who use mentor relationships well ask once, ask precisely, and come back with what they did with the advice.

CAPTION:
"Would you mind taking a quick look at my deck?" feels like a small ask. Sent to five people in one week, it isn't.

Vague asks are expensive for the person answering them - they have to figure out what you actually need before they can even help. Specific asks ("does slide 4's framing land, or does it undersell the market?") get faster, better, more honest answers.

The founders who get the most out of their mentors aren't the ones who ask the most. They're the ones who ask well, and follow up to show what happened next.

That follow-up is the part almost everyone skips, and it's the part that keeps the relationship open for next time.
