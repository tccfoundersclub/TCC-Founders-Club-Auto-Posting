TOPIC:
Remote-first teams lose founder-to-team trust in a specific, avoidable way

HOOK:
The Trust Gap Remote Teams Don't Notice Until It's Already Wide

INTRO:
Everyone blames "remote culture" for distance. Point 2 is the real mechanism.

POINT 1:
In person, trust builds in the small unplanned moments - the hallway comment, the shared laugh before a meeting starts.

POINT 2:
Remote teams lose those moments entirely, not because people don't care, but because nothing replaces them by default.

POINT 3:
The founders who keep remote trust alive build the unplanned moments back in on purpose, instead of assuming the culture will handle itself.

CAPTION:
"Our culture just isn't the same since we went remote" is true, but the reason is more specific than people usually say.

It's not the lack of an office. It's the loss of every small, unplanned moment that used to build trust without anyone trying - the hallway comment, the two minutes before a meeting starts, the read of someone's body language in a hard conversation.

None of that happens by default over Zoom. Teams that stay close remote didn't get lucky - they rebuilt those moments on purpose, with real structure behind the informal stuff.

If your team feels distant, the fix probably isn't more meetings. It's more unplanned ones.
