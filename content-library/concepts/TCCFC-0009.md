TOPIC:
Partnerships fail at the operational handoff, not the pitch

HOOK:
Most Partnerships Don't Die in the Pitch. They Die in the Handoff.

INTRO:
Everyone worries about pitching the partnership. Point 2 is where it actually falls apart.

POINT 1:
Getting a partnership signed is the easy part - both sides are excited and nobody's had to actually do anything yet.

POINT 2:
The handoff after signing is where it usually dies: unclear ownership, no shared timeline, nobody following up first.

POINT 3:
The partnerships that actually produce something have one thing in common - someone specific owned the first 30 days, on both sides.

CAPTION:
The pitch is the easy part of a partnership. Both sides are excited, nobody's had to do any actual work yet, and everyone nods along.

Then it's signed, and there's a silence where the follow-through should be. Nobody's clearly responsible for the first move. Both sides assume the other one will kick things off. Weeks pass. The partnership quietly stops existing.

The partnerships that actually go somewhere have one unglamorous thing in common: someone specific, on each side, owned the first 30 days before the ink was even dry.

If your last few partnerships fizzled, check the handoff before you blame the pitch.
