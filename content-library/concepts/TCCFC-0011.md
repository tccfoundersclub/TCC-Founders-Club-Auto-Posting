Fundraising ease depends more on reputation than pitch quality

HOOK:
Why Some Founders Raise Easily and It Has Almost Nothing to Do With the Pitch

INTRO:
Everyone obsesses over the deck. Point 2 is what investors are actually responding to.

POINT 1:
By the time a strong founder is pitching, the investor has usually already heard about them from someone else first.

POINT 2:
Reputation is built years before the raise, in how you treated people when nothing was at stake.

POINT 3:
The founders who raise easily aren't better presenters. They did the reputation work long before they needed anything from anyone.

CAPTION:
Founders spend weeks polishing a deck and almost no time on the thing that actually predicts whether the raise goes smoothly: what people were already saying about them before the meeting.

By the time a strong founder sits down with an investor, that investor has usually already heard something - from a portfolio founder, an advisor, someone at a dinner six months earlier. The pitch just confirms what the reputation already suggested.

That reputation isn't built during the raise. It's built years earlier, in every interaction where nothing was being asked for.

The best fundraising prep starts long before you need the money.
