TOPIC:
Saying no to the wrong customer is an underused growth strategy

HOOK:
Saying No to the Wrong Customer Is a Growth Strategy, Not a Loss

INTRO:
Every "no" feels like lost revenue. Point 2 is why the best founders don't see it that way.

POINT 1:
Every customer who's a bad fit costs more in support, scope creep, and team morale than the revenue is worth.

POINT 2:
Saying yes to everyone dilutes your product roadmap into a list of one-off requests from people who were never going to be great customers.

POINT 3:
The founders who grow fastest usually got clearer, earlier, about exactly who they don't want as a customer.

CAPTION:
Every "no" to a customer feels like leaving money on the table. In practice, the wrong customer usually costs more than they pay.

Bad-fit customers eat support time, pull your roadmap toward one-off requests that don't serve anyone else, and quietly drain team morale with the friction they bring. It rarely shows up as a clean number, but it's there.

The founders who grow fastest usually aren't the ones who said yes the most. They got specific, early, about exactly who they're building for - and said no more often, on purpose.

Growth isn't just about who you let in. It's about who you don't.
