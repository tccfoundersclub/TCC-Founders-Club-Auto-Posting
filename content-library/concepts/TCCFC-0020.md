An outsider's first impression reveals blind spots founders can't see

HOOK:
The Five Minutes That Tell You More Than Your Last Board Meeting

INTRO:
Founders trust internal feedback. Point 2 is what they're missing.

POINT 1:
Your team is too close to the product to see it the way a first-time user does.

POINT 2:
An outsider's confused first five minutes usually reveals exactly where the product or pitch loses people.

POINT 3:
The founders who improve fastest actively go looking for that confusion instead of waiting for it to show up in churn data.

CAPTION:
Your team can't see your product the way a stranger does. They're too close to it - every confusing part has already been explained to them a hundred times.

A first-time user's confused first five minutes is one of the highest-signal moments you'll get, and most founders never deliberately go looking for it. They wait for it to show up later, as churn, which is the expensive way to learn the same lesson.

The founders who improve fastest actively seek out that fresh, uninformed reaction instead of only listening to people who already know too much.

Go find someone who's never seen it. Watch their face, not just their words.
