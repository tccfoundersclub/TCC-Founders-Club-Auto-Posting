CAPTION:
Building in public gets treated like a marketing tactic. What it actually does is change the speed at which trust compounds.

Founders who only share the wins get polite congratulations from people who don't really know what happened. Founders who share the messy middle - the thing that didn't work, the hard week, the pivot - get something more valuable: people who believe them when it counts.

That trust builds faster in public than in private, simply because more people are watching it happen in real time instead of hearing a polished version secondhand.

It's not about oversharing. It's about being real enough that the wins mean something when they come.
