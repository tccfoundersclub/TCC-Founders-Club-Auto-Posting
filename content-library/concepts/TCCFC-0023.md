TOPIC:
Exit conversations with early employees go wrong in a specific way

HOOK:
Why the Exit Conversation With Your First Employee Almost Never Goes Well

INTRO:
Founders dread it for the wrong reason. Point 2 is what actually makes it go badly.

POINT 1:
Founders prepare for the emotional part of an exit conversation and skip preparing the practical part.

POINT 2:
Vagueness about timeline, equity, and reason is what turns a hard conversation into a resentful one.

POINT 3:
The founders who handle this well go in with specifics decided beforehand, not figured out live in the room.

CAPTION:
Founders brace for the emotional weight of letting an early employee go and forget to prepare the practical side - and it's usually the practical side that turns a hard conversation into a resentful one.

Vague answers about the exact timeline, what happens to equity, or the real reason leave room for the person to fill in the worst version themselves. That's what breeds resentment, not the decision itself.

The founders who handle this well decide the specifics before they walk in - not because they're colder, but because clarity is the kindest thing they can offer in a hard moment.
