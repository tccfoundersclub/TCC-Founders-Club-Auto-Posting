TOPIC:
"We're like family" culture backfires in startups

HOOK:
"We're Like Family Here" Is a Warning Sign, Not a Culture Win

INTRO:
It's meant as a compliment to the team. Point 2 is why it usually creates problems.

POINT 1:
Family relationships don't have clear performance expectations. Employment does, and mixing the two confuses both.

POINT 2:
"We're like family" often gets used to justify unpaid overtime, guilt around leaving, or avoiding hard performance conversations.

POINT 3:
The teams with the healthiest culture treat people like trusted professionals with clear expectations, not like family members who can't leave.

CAPTION:
"We're like family here" is usually said with good intentions. It's also one of the more common phrases behind unpaid overtime, guilt around taking time off, and performance issues nobody addresses because "you don't fire family."

Family relationships don't come with clear expectations or boundaries. Employment does - or should. Blurring the two doesn't make the workplace warmer. It just makes the boundaries fuzzier for everyone, usually in the company's favor.

The healthiest team cultures treat people like trusted professionals with real clarity, not like family members who owe unconditional loyalty.

Care about your team without borrowing language that quietly erases boundaries.
