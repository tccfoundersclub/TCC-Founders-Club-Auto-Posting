TOPIC:
Negotiation skill is preparation, not confidence

HOOK:
Good Negotiators Aren't More Confident. They're More Prepared.

INTRO:
Founders assume negotiation is a personality trait. Point 2 is why that's the wrong lesson.

POINT 1:
Confidence helps you sound sure. It doesn't tell you what your actual walk-away point is.

POINT 2:
Founders who negotiate well have usually decided their numbers and boundaries before the conversation, not during it.

POINT 3:
The most confident-sounding people in a negotiation are often just the most prepared ones in the room.

CAPTION:
Founders watch someone negotiate well and assume it's a personality trait - some people are just naturally confident, some aren't. That's the wrong lesson.

Confidence helps you sound sure in the moment. It doesn't tell you your actual walk-away point, your real priorities, or which terms you can flex on. That only comes from preparation done beforehand, calmly, away from the pressure of the room.

The founders who seem like natural negotiators have usually just done the unglamorous homework first: know your numbers, decide your boundaries, know what you're willing to lose.

Confidence is often just preparation wearing a calmer face.
