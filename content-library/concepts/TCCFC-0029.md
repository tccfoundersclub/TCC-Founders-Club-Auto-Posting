Why Some Co-Founder Friendships Survive a Failed Startup and Most Don't

INTRO:
Everyone assumes it's about how the failure happened. Point 2 is the real predictor.

POINT 1:
It's not the failure itself that ends most co-founder friendships - failures happen to almost everyone eventually.

POINT 2:
What ends the friendship is usually how honestly the two people talked to each other in the six months before it ended.

POINT 3:
The founder pairs who stay close after a shutdown were usually already being direct with each other, long before things got hard.

CAPTION:
A startup failing doesn't automatically end a co-founder friendship. Failure happens to almost everyone eventually - it's not actually the rare or shameful event founders treat it as.

What ends the friendship is usually something that was already broken before the shutdown: months of avoided conversations, resentment that never got said out loud, decisions made without really checking in with each other.

The founder pairs who stay close after closing something down were usually already talking honestly with each other long before it got hard. The failure just revealed what was already true about the relationship.

How you talk to your co-founder now is the actual predictor, not how the ending goes.
