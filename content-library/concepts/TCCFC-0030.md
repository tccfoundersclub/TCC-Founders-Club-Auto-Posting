Instituting a second-opinion call before big decisions

HOOK:
The One Habit That Prevents the Most Regretted Founder Decisions

INTRO:
Most bad calls aren't obvious in the moment. Point 2 is the simple fix.

POINT 1:
Founders rarely regret decisions they talked through with someone first - even if the outcome was still hard.

POINT 2:
Most regretted decisions were made alone, fast, and under pressure, with no outside read before committing.

POINT 3:
The founders who avoid the worst regrets have built a simple habit: one call, one real conversation, before anything big and irreversible.

CAPTION:
Ask founders about their most regretted decisions and a pattern shows up almost every time: they made the call alone, fast, under pressure, without talking it through with anyone first.

It's rarely about the outcome being bad. Even hard outcomes feel different when you know you made the best call you could with the information you had - and talking it through with someone else is usually what gets you there.

The simple fix almost nobody institutionalizes: one real conversation with someone you trust, before anything big and hard to undo. Not a committee. Just one honest outside read.

That single habit prevents more regret than any framework.
