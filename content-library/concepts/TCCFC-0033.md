TOPIC:
Only networking "up" toward more successful people backfires

HOOK:
Only Talking to People More Successful Than You Is a Networking Mistake

INTRO:
It feels like ambition. Point 2 is why it quietly limits you.

POINT 1:
Chasing only the most successful person in the room means you're rarely actually present in the conversation - you're performing for them.

POINT 2:
Peers at your own stage often give you more usable, current advice than someone five stages ahead who's forgotten the details.

POINT 3:
The founders with the strongest networks built relationships across stages, not just upward.

CAPTION:
Always angling to talk to the most successful person in the room feels like ambition. It usually just means you're performing instead of actually connecting - too busy trying to impress to ask a real question.

Someone a few years further along has often forgotten the specific, tactical details of exactly where you are right now. A peer at your own stage usually has more current, usable advice than someone operating five stages ahead.

The strongest networks aren't built by only networking up. They're built across stages - peers, people slightly ahead, people slightly behind - because each gives you something different.
