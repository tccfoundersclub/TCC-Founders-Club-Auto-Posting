HOOK:
Most Founders Wait Until They're Struggling to Look for a Real Peer Group

INTRO:
It's treated like a last resort. Point 2 is why that timing works against you.

POINT 1:
Founders often only start seriously looking for community once things are already going badly.

POINT 2:
Relationships built under stress, for the first time, don't have the trust that relationships built earlier would already have.

POINT 3:
The founders who benefit most from a peer group joined before they needed it, not during the crisis that made them finally look.

CAPTION:
A lot of founders only start seriously looking for a real peer group once something's already going wrong - a bad quarter, a co-founder conflict, a moment that finally makes the isolation feel unbearable.

The problem with that timing: relationships built for the first time under stress don't have the foundation that relationships built earlier would already have. You're trying to build trust and lean on it in the same moment, which is a lot to ask of any new connection.

The founders who get the most out of community joined before they needed it - so that when something hard actually happened, the trust was already there.

Don't wait for the crisis to go looking for your room.
