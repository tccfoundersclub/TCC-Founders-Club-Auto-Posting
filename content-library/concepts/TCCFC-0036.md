TOPIC:
First-time founders systematically under-price their product

HOOK:
The Under-Pricing Mistake Almost Every First-Time Founder Makes

INTRO:
It feels safer to price low and grow into it. Point 2 is why that plan rarely works.

POINT 1:
Pricing low to "grow into it later" sounds reasonable and almost never actually happens - raising prices later is harder than it sounds.

POINT 2:
Low prices don't just mean less revenue. They often attract exactly the price-sensitive customers who complain the most and churn the fastest.

POINT 3:
The founders who price with real confidence usually did the uncomfortable work of testing higher, earlier, instead of assuming the market couldn't bear it.

CAPTION:
"We'll price low now and raise it later" is the plan almost every first-time founder makes, and it almost never plays out the way they picture it. Raising prices on existing customers is far harder than pricing right the first time.

Underpricing doesn't just mean less revenue. It often attracts the most price-sensitive customers in your market - the ones most likely to complain, most likely to churn, least likely to be a good long-term fit.

The founders who price with confidence usually tested higher earlier than felt comfortable, instead of assuming the market would reject it before actually checking.
