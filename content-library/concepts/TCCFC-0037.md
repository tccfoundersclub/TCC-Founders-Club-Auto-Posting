Celebrating a competitor's win changes your own trajectory

HOOK:
The Founders Who Celebrate a Competitor's Win Build Differently Than the Ones Who Resent It

INTRO:
Resentment feels like motivation. Point 2 is what it actually does to your focus.

POINT 1:
Resenting a competitor's win keeps your attention pointed outward, at them, instead of at your own customers.

POINT 2:
Founders who can genuinely celebrate a competitor's success usually have a clearer sense of their own lane, and don't feel threatened by someone else's.

POINT 3:
The energy spent resenting someone else's win is energy not spent building the thing only you can build.

CAPTION:
A competitor raises money or lands a big client, and the instinct for a lot of founders is quiet resentment. It feels like motivation. It's usually just a distraction wearing motivation's clothes.

Resentment keeps your attention pointed at them - what they're doing, why it's unfair, what it means for you - instead of at your own customers and your own roadmap. That's expensive attention to lose.

Founders who can genuinely celebrate someone else's win usually have a clear enough sense of their own lane that another win doesn't feel like a threat to it.

Where your competitor is doing well isn't where your energy needs to go.
