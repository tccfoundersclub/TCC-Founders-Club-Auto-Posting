TOPIC:
Founders have blind spots about their own reputation in a room

HOOK:
You Probably Don't Know What Your Reputation Actually Is in the Rooms You're Not In

INTRO:
Founders assume they know how they're perceived. Point 2 is why that's usually wrong.

POINT 1:
Almost nobody tells a founder directly how they're actually perceived - it happens in rooms they're not in.

POINT 2:
A small comment, a way of following up (or not), a tone in one hard negotiation - these quietly become "how people talk about you."

POINT 3:
The founders who manage their reputation well actively ask trusted people what they're hearing, instead of assuming.

CAPTION:
Almost nobody is going to tell a founder directly how they're actually perceived. That conversation happens in rooms the founder isn't in - after a meeting, at a dinner, in a group chat.

Small things compound into a reputation without the founder ever finding out: how you followed up after a hard negotiation, a comment that came off wrong in a rushed moment, a pattern of canceling on people. None of it feels significant individually. Together, it becomes "how people talk about you."

The founders who manage this well don't assume they know. They actively, occasionally, ask someone they trust what they're actually hearing.
