TOPIC:
Genuine community differs from an audience in a specific way

HOOK:
An Audience Watches You. A Community Does Something Together.

INTRO:
Founders often build the first and call it the second. Point 2 is the actual difference.

POINT 1:
An audience is people who follow what you post. A community is people who know and rely on each other, not just you.

POINT 2:
Founders often measure "community" by follower count, which is really just measuring audience size.

POINT 3:
The founders building real community can name specific relationships that exist between two members - not just between each member and the founder.

CAPTION:
A lot of founders say they're "building a community" when what they've actually built is an audience - people who follow along, react, maybe comment, but don't know each other and wouldn't recognize each other in a room.

The test is simple: can you name relationships that exist between two members, independent of you? If every connection in the group runs through the founder, it's an audience with a community label on it.

Real community happens when members start relying on each other - when the value isn't just "content from the founder" but "people I trust who happen to be here too."

That's a much harder, much more valuable thing to build.
