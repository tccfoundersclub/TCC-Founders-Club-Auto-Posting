Founders overestimate how much their network can be built through content alone

HOOK:
Posting Good Content Doesn't Build a Network. It Builds an Impression.

INTRO:
Founders think visibility equals relationships. Point 2 is the gap between the two.

POINT 1:
A good post gets likes and comments from people who now know of you. That's not the same as people who know you.

POINT 2:
Real relationships need a two-way exchange - a conversation, a favor, a shared room - not just one-way visibility.

POINT 3:
The founders with genuinely strong networks used content to open doors, then walked through them with real conversations.

CAPTION:
A good LinkedIn or Instagram post can get a lot of engagement and create almost zero actual relationship. People now know of you. That's not the same as people who know you.

A network is built on two-way exchange - an actual conversation, a favor given or received, time spent in the same room. Content is one-way. It's a great door-opener and a terrible substitute for what happens after the door opens.

The founders with genuinely strong networks used their visibility to get the conversation started, then did the unglamorous work of actually following through in person.

Content gets you noticed. Relationships get you helped.
