TOPIC:
Founders assume their team already knows the company's why

HOOK:
Your Team Probably Doesn't Know "Why" as Clearly as You Think They Do

INTRO:
Founders live the mission daily. Point 2 is why that closeness is misleading.

POINT 1:
A founder repeats the company's why constantly in their own head, which makes it feel obvious to everyone else too. It isn't.

POINT 2:
Most employees can describe what the company does. Far fewer can clearly explain why it matters, in their own words.

POINT 3:
The founders who keep teams aligned through hard stretches repeat the why far more often than feels necessary to them personally.

CAPTION:
Founders live inside the company's "why" every single day - it's in their head constantly, which makes it feel completely obvious to everyone else too. It usually isn't.

Ask most teams what the company does and you'll get a clear answer. Ask why it actually matters, in their own words, and the answers get vague fast. That gap is where motivation quietly leaks out during hard stretches.

The founders who keep teams aligned through the tough months repeat the why far more often than feels necessary to them personally - almost to the point of feeling repetitive. That repetition is doing real work.
