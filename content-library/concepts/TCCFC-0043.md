TOPIC:
Founders under-invest in documenting decisions, causing repeated debates

HOOK:
Why Your Team Keeps Re-Litigating the Same Decision Every Quarter

INTRO:
It feels like the team can't commit. Point 2 is the actual cause.

POINT 1:
A decision made in one meeting, with no written record of why, is a decision that will get questioned again in three months.

POINT 2:
Without documented reasoning, people fill the gap with their own guess at "why we did it that way" - and guesses drift.

POINT 3:
The founders who stop the re-litigating write down the reasoning once, briefly, and point back to it instead of re-arguing from scratch.

CAPTION:
Teams that keep re-debating the same decision aren't indecisive. They're usually just missing a written record of why the decision was made the first time.

Without that record, everyone's operating on their own memory of the meeting - and memories drift fast. Three months later, half the team half-remembers the reasoning, and the debate starts over from zero.

The founders who stop this pattern write the "why" down once, briefly, right after the decision. Not a long document - a few sentences. Then, next time it comes up, they point back to it instead of re-arguing the whole thing.

Fewer meetings. Same decision, once.
