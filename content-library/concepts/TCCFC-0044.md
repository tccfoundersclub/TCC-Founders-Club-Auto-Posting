TOPIC:
Founders treat customer churn as a product problem when it's often a relationship problem

HOOK:
Churn Isn't Always a Product Problem. Sometimes It's a Relationship Problem.

INTRO:
Founders default to fixing features when customers leave. Point 2 is the angle they miss.

POINT 1:
A customer who never hears from you after signing up has no relationship to lose, so leaving costs them nothing.

POINT 2:
Product fixes address complaints. They don't address the customer who left quietly, without complaining, because no one ever checked in.

POINT 3:
The founders with the lowest churn treat the relationship as something to maintain, not just the product.

CAPTION:
When churn ticks up, the instinct is almost always to look at the product - what's broken, what's missing, what needs fixing. Sometimes that's right. Often, the bigger issue is that nobody ever built a relationship with the customer in the first place.

A customer who signs up and never hears from you again has nothing invested in staying. Leaving costs them nothing, because there was never really a relationship to leave.

The founders with the lowest churn aren't always running the best product. They're the ones who kept checking in, kept the relationship warm, and made staying feel like it mattered to someone.
