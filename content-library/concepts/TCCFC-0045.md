TOPIC:
Founders confuse consensus-seeking with good leadership

HOOK:
Seeking Consensus on Everything Isn't Good Leadership. It's Often the Absence of It.

INTRO:
It feels inclusive. Point 2 is what it actually costs a team.

POINT 1:
Consensus feels collaborative, but on most real decisions, someone in the room has to actually decide and own it.

POINT 2:
Endless consensus-seeking usually means the founder is avoiding the discomfort of a call that might upset someone.

POINT 3:
The founders teams trust most gather input generously and still make the final call clearly, without hiding behind "the group decided."

CAPTION:
Asking for everyone's input feels inclusive, and it is - up to a point. Past that point, constantly seeking full consensus is often just a founder avoiding the discomfort of making a call that might upset someone.

Teams don't actually want a founder who defers every decision to the group. They want someone who listens well and then decides clearly, and takes responsibility for that decision instead of hiding behind "well, that's what everyone agreed on."

The founders who build the most trust gather input generously and still own the final call, visibly, as theirs.

Input is a gift. The decision is still your job.
