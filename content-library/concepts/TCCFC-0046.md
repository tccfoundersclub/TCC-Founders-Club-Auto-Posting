INTRO:

POINT 1:
A bad first launch feels catastrophic in the moment and is almost never remembered a year later.

POINT 2:
What actually gets remembered is what you did in the weeks after - whether you listened, iterated, and kept showing up.

CAPTION:
Launch day feels like it decides everything. In the moment, a quiet launch or a bug-filled first week can feel like proof the whole idea was wrong.

A year later, almost nobody remembers the launch. What they remember - what customers remember - is what happened in the weeks after: did you listen when something didn't work, did you fix it fast, did you keep showing up.

The founders who build things that last treat launch day as one data point among many, not a final verdict. The real story gets written in what comes after.

If your launch didn't go the way you hoped, the story isn't over. It just started.
