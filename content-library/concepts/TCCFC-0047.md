TOPIC:
Founders avoid asking existing customers directly for referrals

HOOK:
The Referral You Never Got Because You Never Actually Asked

INTRO:
Founders wait for referrals to happen naturally. Point 2 is why that's a slow strategy.

POINT 1:
Happy customers rarely refer you spontaneously - they're busy, and it's not top of mind even when they love the product.

POINT 2:
A direct, specific ask ("do you know one other founder who'd get value from this?") gets results that waiting never does.

POINT 3:
The founders with the strongest referral engines built a simple habit of asking, every time a customer expressed real satisfaction.

CAPTION:
Happy customers rarely refer you on their own, even when they genuinely love what you built. They're busy, it's not top of mind, and "tell a friend" isn't something people do spontaneously just because a product worked well.

The fix is almost embarrassingly simple and most founders skip it: ask directly. Not a vague "let us know if you know anyone" - a specific ask, right when a customer expresses real satisfaction, for one specific kind of person.

The founders with the strongest referral engines aren't running clever campaigns. They just built the habit of asking, consistently, at the right moment.
