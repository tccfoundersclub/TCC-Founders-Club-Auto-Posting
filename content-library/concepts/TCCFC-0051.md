TOPIC:
Founders neglect their own onboarding when joining a room or community

HOOK:
Showing Up to a New Room and Waiting to Be Noticed Rarely Works

INTRO:
Founders join communities passively. Point 2 is why that approach underperforms.

POINT 1:
Joining a new group and waiting for people to approach you puts your outcome entirely in someone else's hands.

POINT 2:
The founders who get real value from a new room are usually the ones who introduced themselves first, more than once.

POINT 3:
A room only works for you as much as you actively work it - showing up isn't the same as participating.

CAPTION:
Joining a new founder community and quietly waiting to be noticed is a common instinct, and it rarely produces much. You've put your entire outcome in the hands of other people's initiative.

The founders who get real value out of a new room are the ones who did the uncomfortable thing early: introduced themselves first, asked someone to grab coffee, followed up after a first conversation instead of waiting for the other person to.

Membership isn't participation. A room only gives you back what you actually put into engaging with it.
