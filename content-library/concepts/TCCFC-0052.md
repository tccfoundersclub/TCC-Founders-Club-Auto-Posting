TOPIC:
Founders treat every piece of feedback as equally important

HOOK:
Not All Feedback Deserves the Same Weight, and Treating It That Way Will Exhaust You

INTRO:
Every comment feels like it needs a response. Point 2 is why that instinct burns founders out.

POINT 1:
A one-off comment from a random user and a repeated pattern from ten customers are not the same signal, even though both feel urgent in the moment.

POINT 2:
Founders who react to every piece of feedback equally end up building a product shaped by whoever complained loudest, not by real patterns.

POINT 3:
The founders who filter well ask "is this one voice or a pattern?" before deciding how much weight to give it.

CAPTION:
Every piece of feedback lands with the same emotional urgency in the moment, and that's exactly the trap. A single frustrated comment and a pattern echoed by ten different customers feel similarly loud, but they're not the same signal.

Founders who treat all feedback equally end up building a product shaped by whoever complained the most recently or the loudest, instead of by what actually matters to most of their customers.

The founders who filter well pause before reacting and ask one question: is this one voice, or a pattern? That single question changes almost every roadmap decision that follows.
