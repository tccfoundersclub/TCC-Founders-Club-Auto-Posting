TOPIC:
Founders avoid setting explicit expectations with early investors about communication

HOOK:
The Awkward Investor Update Conversation You Never Had Is Costing You Later

INTRO:
Founders assume investors know what to expect. Point 2 is why that assumption backfires.

POINT 1:
Without an explicit agreement on update frequency, investors fill the silence with their own assumptions - usually worse than reality.

POINT 2:
A founder who goes quiet for months isn't necessarily failing, but silence reads as failing if nobody set expectations upfront.

POINT 3:
The founders with the calmest investor relationships set the communication rhythm explicitly, early, before the pressure sets in.

CAPTION:
Most founders never explicitly agree with investors on how often they'll hear from you. It feels like an unnecessary formality in an exciting first meeting. Then six months of silence goes by during a hard stretch, and the investor fills that silence with their own worst assumptions.

Going quiet doesn't automatically mean things are failing. But it reads that way when there was never an explicit understanding of what "normal" communication looks like.

The founders with the calmest investor relationships set that rhythm early - monthly update, quarterly call, whatever it is - before the pressure of a hard quarter makes it feel like a confession to reach out.
