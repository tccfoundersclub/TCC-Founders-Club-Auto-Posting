TOPIC:
Founders underestimate the value of a founder who has already failed once

HOOK:
Why the Most Useful Person in the Room Might Be the Founder Who Already Failed

INTRO:
Founders chase advice from people currently succeeding. Point 2 is who else is worth listening to.

POINT 1:
A founder who's currently succeeding can tell you what's working for them right now. That's useful, but it's a narrow view.

POINT 2:
A founder who's already been through a shutdown has usually done more honest reflection on what actually mattered than anyone still riding a win.

POINT 3:
The rooms with the most useful advice usually have a mix - not just people currently winning, but people who've already lost and looked closely at why.

CAPTION:
It's natural to want advice from the founder who's currently winning - the recent raise, the growth chart everyone's talking about. That advice is useful, but it's also narrow: it's one person's story, mid-story, without the benefit of hindsight.

A founder who's already been through a shutdown has usually done something rarer: honest, painful reflection on exactly what mattered and what didn't, with nothing left to protect by pretending otherwise.

The most useful rooms aren't full of only winners. They mix people currently succeeding with people who've already failed and looked closely at why - because both perspectives are incomplete alone.
