TOPIC:
Founders avoid discussing money openly with peers, which limits useful benchmarking

HOOK:
Founders Don't Talk About Money With Each Other Enough, and It's Costing Everyone

INTRO:
Money stays private even among close founder friends. Point 2 is what that silence costs.

POINT 1:
Without real numbers to compare against, founders default to guessing whether their pricing, burn, or salaries are reasonable.

POINT 2:
A vague sense of "we're probably fine" is a worse decision-making tool than one honest conversation with a peer at a similar stage.

POINT 3:
The founders who make sharper financial decisions usually have at least one or two peers they talk numbers with candidly.

CAPTION:
Founders will talk about almost anything with their peers except actual numbers - revenue, burn, salaries, what they pay themselves. It stays private even among people who are otherwise genuinely close.

The cost of that silence is real: without anything to compare against, founders end up guessing whether their pricing is reasonable, their burn is normal, their own salary is fair. Guessing is a worse decision tool than one honest conversation.

The founders who make sharper financial calls usually have at least one or two peers they can talk numbers with candidly, without either side making it weird.
