POINT 1:
A recurring meeting started for a real reason six months ago often outlives that reason without anyone noticing.

POINT 2:

POINT 3:
The founders who protect real productivity audit recurring meetings regularly and kill the ones that have quietly stopped earning their slot.

CAPTION:
Almost every team has at least one recurring meeting that started for a good reason months ago and has been running on autopilot ever since, long after that reason stopped applying.

It doesn't feel like a big deal - it's just one hour. But multiply that hour by everyone in the room, every week, and it's a meaningful chunk of the team's focused time, spent on something nobody would actually choose to start today.

The founders who protect real productivity don't just add meetings when a need arises. They periodically ask which recurring ones have quietly stopped earning their spot on the calendar - and kill them.
