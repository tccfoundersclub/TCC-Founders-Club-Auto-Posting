TOPIC:
Founders assume their story is compelling without ever practicing telling it

HOOK:
You've Never Actually Practiced Telling Your Own Founder Story Out Loud

INTRO:
Founders assume their story is naturally compelling. Point 2 is why that assumption often fails in the room.

POINT 1:
Living your founder story every day makes it feel obvious to you - and obvious things are easy to tell badly, without noticing.

POINT 2:
A founder story told for the first time, live, in front of an investor or a room, usually comes out rambling and unfocused.

POINT 3:
The founders whose story lands every time have said it out loud, deliberately, enough times that the version has been sharpened by repetition.

CAPTION:
Most founders have never actually rehearsed telling their own story out loud. It's lived inside their head so long that it feels obvious, and obvious things are surprisingly easy to tell badly the first few times you say them to someone else.

The result: a founder story that comes out rambling in the exact moment it needs to land - a pitch, an interview, an investor meeting - because the first real attempt at telling it happened live, under pressure.

The founders whose story consistently lands have said it out loud enough times, in low-stakes settings, that the sharpest version has already been found before it matters.
