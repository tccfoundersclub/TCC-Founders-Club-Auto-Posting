TOPIC:
Founders underestimate the compounding value of showing up consistently to the same room

HOOK:
Showing Up to the Same Room Ten Times Beats Ten Different Rooms Once

INTRO:
Founders chase variety in networking. Point 2 is why consistency usually wins.

POINT 1:
A new room every time means starting from zero trust, over and over, with no compounding.

POINT 2:
The same room, visited repeatedly, builds context and familiarity that turns strangers into people who'll actually help you.

POINT 3:
The founders with the deepest relationships picked a smaller number of rooms and kept showing up, instead of spreading thin across many.

CAPTION:
It's tempting to chase variety - a new event, a new group, a new room every week, trying to maximize exposure. Each one starts you back at zero trust, with people who don't know you yet and won't remember you by the next event.

Showing up to the same room ten times works differently. People start to recognize you, remember the last conversation, build context about what you're working on. That familiarity is what eventually turns into real help - an intro, an opportunity, honest advice.

The founders with the deepest, most useful relationships usually picked fewer rooms and stayed consistent, instead of spreading themselves across many rooms once each.
