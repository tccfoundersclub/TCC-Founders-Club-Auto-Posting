TOPIC:
Founders avoid setting a clear decision-making process for co-founder disagreements

HOOK:
What Happens When You and Your Co-Founder Disagree? Most Founders Don't Actually Know.

INTRO:
The question feels theoretical until it isn't. Point 2 is why answering it early matters.

POINT 1:
Most co-founder teams have never explicitly agreed on what happens when they genuinely disagree on something important.

POINT 2:
Without a process, disagreements get resolved by whoever's more persistent or louder in the moment, not by what's actually right.

POINT 3:
The founder pairs who navigate conflict well decided the process - who has final say on what, how ties get broken - before they needed it.

CAPTION:
Ask most co-founder pairs "what happens when you two genuinely disagree on something big?" and you'll get a shrug. It's never come up explicitly - they've just muddled through it each time it happened.

Without an agreed process, disagreements tend to get resolved by whoever's more persistent, more senior-feeling, or louder in that specific conversation. That's not the same as resolving them by what's actually right for the company.

The founder pairs who handle conflict well decided this in advance - who has final say in which domains, how a genuine tie gets broken - before the pressure of an actual disagreement made it harder to think clearly.
