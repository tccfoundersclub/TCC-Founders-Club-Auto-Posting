TOPIC:
Founders assume a good mentor relationship happens automatically after one good meeting

HOOK:
One Good Mentor Meeting Doesn't Make a Mentor Relationship

INTRO:
Founders treat a strong first conversation as the whole relationship. Point 2 is what's missing.

POINT 1:
A great first conversation with a potential mentor creates goodwill. It doesn't create an ongoing relationship on its own.

POINT 2:
Relationships that actually function need a second touchpoint that the founder initiates, not one they wait for.

POINT 3:
The founders who build real mentor relationships follow up with something specific - an update, a result, a new question - not just "let's stay in touch."

CAPTION:
A genuinely great first conversation with a potential mentor feels like the start of something real. Often, it's the whole relationship - one good meeting that never turns into anything else, because nobody followed up with intention.

Goodwill from a strong first conversation fades fast if nothing specific happens next. "Let's stay in touch" is a polite closing line, not an actual plan.

The founders who build lasting mentor relationships are the ones who follow up with something concrete - here's what I did with your advice, here's a new specific question - and keep doing that, consistently, over time.

One good meeting is an opening. The relationship gets built after it.
