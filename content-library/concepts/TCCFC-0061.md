TOPIC:
Founders overvalue their own product knowledge when hiring for sales/marketing roles

HOOK:
Knowing Your Product Isn't the Hardest Part of Selling It

INTRO:
Founders assume deep product knowledge is the top hiring priority for sales. Point 2 is what they underweight.

POINT 1:
Product knowledge can be taught in weeks. The instinct for reading a room and knowing when to push or pull back can't be taught nearly as fast.

POINT 2:
Founders who over-index on product expertise in a sales hire often end up with someone who explains well and closes poorly.

POINT 3:
The founders who build strong sales teams hire for judgment and curiosity first, and treat product depth as something that comes with time.

CAPTION:
Founders hiring their first salesperson often prioritize deep product knowledge above almost everything else. It feels like the safest, most obvious requirement.

Product knowledge is genuinely learnable in a matter of weeks. What's much harder to teach is the instinct for reading a room, knowing exactly when to push and when to back off, and genuine curiosity about the person on the other side of the call. Those either exist in someone or they don't, mostly.

The founders who build strong sales teams hire for that judgment first and let product depth build naturally over the first month. The reverse - product experts who never quite learn to sell - is a much harder gap to close.
