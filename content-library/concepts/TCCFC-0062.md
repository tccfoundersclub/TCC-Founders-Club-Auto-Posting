TOPIC:
Founders assume their team can read their mind about priorities during crunch time

HOOK:
Your Team Can't Read Your Mind About What Actually Matters Right Now

INTRO:
During a crunch, founders assume priorities are obvious. Point 2 is why they usually aren't.

POINT 1:
What feels obviously urgent to a founder, who sees the whole picture, is rarely obvious to someone only seeing their own part of it.

POINT 2:
Without explicit, repeated communication about what matters most right now, teams spread effort evenly across everything - including the things that don't matter as much.

POINT 3:
The founders who navigate crunch periods well over-communicate priority, even past the point where it feels repetitive to them.

CAPTION:
In a crunch, founders often assume the priority is obvious - of course everyone knows what matters most right now. It's obvious to the founder, who sees the entire picture. It's rarely as obvious to someone who only sees their own slice of the work.

Without explicit, repeated communication, teams under pressure tend to spread their effort evenly across everything on their plate, including things that matter far less than the one or two priorities that actually count.

The founders who lead well through crunch periods say the priority out loud more often than feels necessary to them - past the point of feeling repetitive - because repetitive is what it actually takes to land.
