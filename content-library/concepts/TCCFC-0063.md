TOPIC:
Founders neglect to close the loop with people who gave advice that didn't get used

HOOK:
The Advice You Didn't Take Still Deserves a Follow-Up

INTRO:
Founders follow up when advice worked. Point 2 is the follow-up they usually skip.

POINT 1:
Circling back to say "that advice worked great" is easy and most founders do it naturally.

POINT 2:
Circling back to explain why you went a different direction is much rarer, and it's what actually builds long-term trust.

POINT 3:
The founders with the strongest advisor relationships close the loop either way, not just when the news is good.

CAPTION:
When advice works out, most founders naturally circle back to say thanks and share the result. It feels good to deliver good news.

When the advice wasn't taken, or was tried and didn't fit, that follow-up almost never happens. The conversation just quietly ends, and the person who gave the advice has no idea what became of it.

That's a missed opportunity. Explaining why you went a different direction - respectfully, with real reasoning - shows the person their input was actually considered, not just politely received. That's what builds trust that lasts past one conversation.

Close the loop either way. It matters more than the outcome itself.
