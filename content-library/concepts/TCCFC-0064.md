TOPIC:
Founders underestimate how much clarity about company values prevents future conflict

HOOK:
The Values Conversation You Skipped Is Showing Up as Conflict Now

INTRO:
Founders treat "company values" as a branding exercise. Point 2 is what they actually prevent.

POINT 1:
Vague or skipped values conversations don't disappear - they just show up later as disagreements that seem to be about something else entirely.

POINT 2:
A hiring decision, a customer call, a hard performance conversation - these all get easier when there's a real, specific shared standard to point to.

POINT 3:
The founders who invest early in genuinely specific values (not generic posters) spend far less time arguing about things that values would have already settled.

CAPTION:
"Company values" gets treated like a branding exercise - a few words on a wall, mostly for show. Skipping the real version of that conversation doesn't make the underlying questions go away. It just means they show up later, disguised as arguments about something else.

A disagreement about how to handle a difficult customer, or whether to hire someone who's talented but difficult, often traces back to a values question that was never actually settled as a team.

The founders who do this work early - real, specific values, not generic posters - spend noticeably less time re-litigating decisions that a shared standard would have already resolved.
