TOPIC:
Founders assume growth is linear and get discouraged by plateaus

HOOK:
Growth Isn't a Straight Line, and Expecting One Is Setting You Up to Quit

INTRO:
Founders picture a steady upward curve. Point 2 is what actually happens instead.

POINT 1:
Real growth comes in stretches of visible progress followed by long, flat stretches that feel like nothing's happening.

POINT 2:
Founders who expect a straight line interpret the flat stretch as failure, when it's usually just the normal shape of the process.

POINT 3:
The founders who push through plateaus have usually seen the pattern before, so they recognize it instead of panicking about it.

CAPTION:
Most founders picture growth as a steady climb - a little better every week, in a straight line. Actual growth almost never looks like that.

It comes in bursts: real, visible progress, followed by a flat stretch that can last weeks or months and feels like nothing is happening at all. That flat stretch isn't failure. It's usually just the normal, unglamorous shape of the process - the part right before the next jump.

Founders who expect the straight line panic during the flat stretch and sometimes quit right before things would have moved again. The founders who push through have usually seen this pattern before, and recognize it instead of reading it as proof they were wrong.
