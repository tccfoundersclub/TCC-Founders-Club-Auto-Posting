TOPIC:
Founders overlook the compounding value of small consistent community contributions

HOOK:
You Don't Need a Big Gesture to Matter in a Room. You Need Consistency.

INTRO:
Founders think standing out requires a big move. Point 2 is what actually builds a reputation in a community.

POINT 1:
A single impressive gesture is memorable for a week and forgotten by the next month.

POINT 2:
Small, consistent contributions - showing up, answering a question, following up on someone's ask - compound into "that's a person I trust" over time.

POINT 3:
The founders with the strongest reputations in a community built it through repetition, not through one standout moment.

CAPTION:
It's tempting to think you need a big, impressive gesture to matter in a room - the standout comment, the generous favor that gets noticed. Those moments are nice. They're also mostly forgotten within a month.

What actually builds a reputation in a community is smaller and less glamorous: showing up consistently, answering someone's question, following up on something you said you'd do. None of it is memorable on its own. All of it compounds.

The founders with the strongest reputations in any room got there through repetition, not through one moment anyone remembers specifically.
