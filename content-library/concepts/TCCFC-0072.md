TOPIC:
Founders equate business growth with personal worth, which distorts decisions

HOOK:
Your Business's Growth Rate Is Not a Measurement of Your Worth as a Person

INTRO:
Founders quietly conflate the two. Point 2 is what that conflation does to decision-making.

POINT 1:
When the business is treated as a proxy for self-worth, every metric dip starts to feel like a personal indictment.

POINT 2:
That emotional weight leads to reactive decisions made from fear, not from clear-headed strategy.

POINT 3:
The founders who make the calmest decisions have separated "how's the business doing" from "am I okay" - even though it's genuinely hard to do.

CAPTION:
Somewhere along the way, a lot of founders start quietly treating their business's growth rate as a scoreboard for their own worth as a person. Nobody says it out loud. It shows up in how a bad month feels - not like a data point, but like a personal indictment.

That emotional weight distorts decision-making. Fear-driven choices made to fix a feeling of inadequacy are rarely the same choices a clear head would make for the business.

The founders who stay level-headed through hard stretches have done real work separating "how is the business doing" from "am I okay as a person" - two different questions that get tangled together far too easily.
