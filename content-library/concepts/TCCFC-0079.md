TOPIC:
Founders underestimate how quickly a strong culture can erode without maintenance

HOOK:
A Great Culture Doesn't Stay Great on Its Own. It Needs Maintenance Like Anything Else.

INTRO:
Founders assume a good culture, once built, is stable. Point 2 is why that assumption is risky.

POINT 1:
Culture isn't a one-time achievement - it's maintained daily, in a hundred small decisions about who gets hired and what gets tolerated.

POINT 2:
A single bad hire, unaddressed, can quietly shift what feels normal for everyone else on the team.

POINT 3:
The founders who protect a strong culture over years treat it as ongoing work, not something they built once and can stop thinking about.

CAPTION:
Founders who've built a genuinely strong culture sometimes relax about it, treating it like a finished achievement - we built a great culture, now we can focus on other things. Culture doesn't actually work that way.

It's maintained daily, in a hundred small decisions: who gets hired, what behavior gets addressed versus quietly tolerated, what gets celebrated. A single bad hire left unaddressed can shift what feels "normal" for the whole team faster than most founders expect.

The founders who protect a strong culture over years treat it as ongoing, active work - not a milestone they hit once and can stop paying attention to.
