TOPIC:
Founders overlook the value of teaching what they know to junior founders

HOOK:
Teaching a Newer Founder What You Know Sharpens It More Than You'd Expect

INTRO:
Founders treat mentoring as one-directional generosity. Point 2 is what they get back.

POINT 1:
Explaining something you "just know" to someone earlier in the journey forces you to actually articulate the reasoning, not just the instinct.

POINT 2:
That articulation often reveals gaps in your own thinking you hadn't noticed while just doing the thing on autopilot.

POINT 3:
The founders who mentor consistently often report it sharpened their own decision-making, not just the person they were helping.

CAPTION:
Mentoring a newer founder gets framed as pure generosity - giving your time and experience to someone earlier in the journey, expecting nothing back. There's something you do get back, and it's underrated.

Explaining something you've internalized as pure instinct forces you to actually articulate the reasoning behind it. That process regularly reveals gaps in your own thinking - places where you were doing the right thing without fully understanding why, until you had to explain it to someone else.

The founders who mentor consistently often say it sharpened their own decision-making as much as it helped the person on the other side.
