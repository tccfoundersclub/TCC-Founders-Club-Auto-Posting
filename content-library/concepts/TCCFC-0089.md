TOPIC:
Founders overvalue investor validation over customer validation

HOOK:
An Investor Saying Yes Doesn't Mean a Customer Will

INTRO:
Founders chase investor enthusiasm as proof of an idea. Point 2 is why that proof is weaker than it feels.

POINT 1:
Investor excitement is based on a story about the future. Customer behavior is based on what's actually happening now.

POINT 2:
A founder can raise a round on a compelling narrative and still discover, months later, that customers don't behave the way the narrative assumed.

POINT 3:
The founders building the most durable businesses treat customer validation as the real signal, and investor interest as a downstream result of it, not a substitute for it.

CAPTION:
Getting an investor excited feels like validation - someone smart and experienced believed the story enough to write a check. It's real, and it's also a different kind of signal than customers actually behaving the way your business needs them to.

Investor enthusiasm is a bet on a story about the future. Customer behavior is evidence about right now. A founder can raise a strong round on a compelling narrative and still discover months later that the underlying customer behavior doesn't match it.

The founders building the most durable businesses treat customer validation as the real foundation, and let investor interest follow from that - not the other way around.
