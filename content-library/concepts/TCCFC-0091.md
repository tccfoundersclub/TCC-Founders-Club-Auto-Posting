TOPIC:
Founders wait for permission to make obvious calls

HOOK:
You Don't Need Permission to Make the Call You Already Know Is Right

INTRO:
Founders look for outside validation before acting. Point 2 is what that habit costs.

POINT 1:
Waiting for an investor or a peer to bless an obvious decision just delays a call you'd already made internally.

POINT 2:
Seeking permission often isn't about getting better information - it's about spreading the responsibility if it goes wrong.

POINT 3:
The founders who move fastest make the obvious calls themselves and use outside input for the genuinely uncertain ones, not every one.

CAPTION:
A decision that's already obvious internally sometimes still gets delayed while a founder quietly looks for an investor's blessing, a peer's agreement, some external permission before acting on it.

That delay usually isn't about needing better information - the decision was already clear. It's about wanting to spread the responsibility in case it goes wrong. Understandable, and expensive in time.

The founders who move fastest reserve outside input for decisions that are genuinely uncertain, and just make the obvious calls themselves, promptly, without waiting for someone else to say it's okay.
