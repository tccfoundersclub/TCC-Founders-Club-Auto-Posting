TOPIC:
Over-apologizing in negotiations weakens a founder's position

HOOK:
Every "Sorry" in a Negotiation Is Quietly Giving Something Away

INTRO:
It feels polite. Point 2 is what it actually signals to the other side.

POINT 1:
Apologizing for asking for fair terms reframes a reasonable request as an imposition, even when it isn't one.

POINT 2:
The other side reads over-apologizing as uncertainty, and uncertainty invites them to push harder.

POINT 3:
The founders who negotiate well stay warm and respectful without apologizing for having reasonable needs.

CAPTION:
"Sorry, I know this is a lot to ask, but..." feels polite. In a negotiation, it's quietly doing something else - reframing a completely reasonable request as an imposition, before the other side has even reacted to it.

People read over-apologizing as uncertainty, and uncertainty is an invitation to push a little harder, because it signals the ask might have room to give.

The founders who negotiate well stay warm and respectful without apologizing for having fair, reasonable needs. Kindness and confidence aren't opposites - the best negotiators use both at once.
