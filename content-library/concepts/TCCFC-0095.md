TOPIC:
Confusing agreeableness with being a good team player

HOOK:
Being Agreeable Isn't the Same as Being a Good Team Player, and Confusing Them Costs You

INTRO:
Founders often reward agreement. Point 2 is what that habit quietly filters out.

POINT 1:
A team member who never pushes back can feel easy to work with and rarely improves the decisions being made.

POINT 2:
Founders who reward agreeableness, even unconsciously, slowly train their sharpest people to stop disagreeing.

POINT 3:
The strongest teams have a culture where pushing back respectfully is treated as more valuable than simply going along.

CAPTION:
A team member who agrees easily, rarely pushes back, and makes meetings feel smooth is pleasant to work with. Pleasant isn't the same as valuable - that same dynamic rarely improves the actual decisions being made.

Founders who reward agreeableness, even without meaning to - a warmer reaction to "sounds great" than to "have you considered..." - slowly train their sharpest people to stop disagreeing out loud. The disagreement doesn't disappear. It just goes underground.

The strongest teams build a culture where respectful pushback is treated as more valuable than simple agreement, because that's what actually catches problems before they become expensive ones.
