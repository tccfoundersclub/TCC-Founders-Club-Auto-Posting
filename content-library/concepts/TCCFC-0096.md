TOPIC:
Hiring senior people too early, before systems exist to support them

HOOK:
Hiring a Senior Leader Before You Have Any Systems for Them to Lead Is a Common, Expensive Mistake

INTRO:
It feels like buying maturity early. Point 2 is why it often backfires.

POINT 1:
A senior hire brought in to bring structure needs some baseline of process to actually build on - without it, they're improvising from zero, just like everyone else.

POINT 2:
Senior people who thrive at scale often struggle in true chaos, and founders sometimes discover that mismatch only after the hire.

POINT 3:
The founders who time senior hires well build a minimum viable system first, then bring in someone who can take it further, not someone expected to invent it from nothing.

CAPTION:
Bringing in a senior leader early can feel like a shortcut - buy the maturity and structure instead of building it slowly yourself. It often backfires, because most senior hires are strongest at scaling and refining systems, not inventing them from absolute zero.

Dropped into real chaos with no baseline process to build on, a strong senior hire ends up improvising just like everyone else, and the specific expertise they were hired for doesn't get to show up yet.

The founders who time this well build a rough, minimum version of the system themselves first, then bring in someone senior to take it further - not to invent it from nothing.
