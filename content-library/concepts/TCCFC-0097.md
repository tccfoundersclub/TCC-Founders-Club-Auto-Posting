TOPIC:
Changing the pitch or story too often costs founders credibility

HOOK:
Changing Your Story Every Few Months Is Quietly Costing You Credibility

INTRO:
Iterating the narrative feels smart. Point 2 is the cost nobody tracks.

POINT 1:
Refining the pitch as you learn more makes sense. Changing the fundamental story every few months looks like uncertainty to anyone watching closely.

POINT 2:
Investors, partners, and early customers who've heard three different versions of "what we're building" start to lose confidence in the direction, even if each version was individually reasonable.

POINT 3:
The founders who keep credibility high iterate the details while keeping the core story stable, and treat major pivots as rare, well-explained events.

CAPTION:
Founders often iterate their pitch as they learn more, which is healthy. The trouble starts when the fundamental story - not the details, the actual core narrative of what the company is - changes every few months.

Anyone paying close attention, an investor, a longtime customer, an early employee, notices. Three different versions of "what we're building" in a year reads as uncertainty, even if each version made sense on its own at the time.

The founders who keep credibility high iterate the surface constantly and keep the core story stable, treating a genuine pivot as a rare, deliberate, well-explained event rather than a frequent adjustment.
