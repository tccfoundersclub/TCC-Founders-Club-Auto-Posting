TOPIC:
Founders overestimate how much a rebrand fixes underlying problems

HOOK:
A Rebrand Won't Fix What's Actually Broken

INTRO:
A new look feels like fresh momentum. Point 2 is what it doesn't touch.

POINT 1:
A new logo and website feel like progress because they're visible and fast to execute.

POINT 2:
If the underlying issue is retention, sales process, or product fit, a rebrand changes the packaging, not the problem.

POINT 3:
The founders who fix real issues diagnose the actual constraint first, and treat brand work as separate from that fix, not a substitute for it.

CAPTION:
A rebrand feels like momentum - new logo, new site, new energy. It's visible, it's fast to execute, and it feels like real progress compared to the slower, harder work of fixing what's actually broken underneath.

If the real issue is retention, an unclear sales process, or a product that doesn't quite fit the market, a new look changes the packaging and leaves the problem exactly where it was, just better dressed.

The founders who solve real problems diagnose the actual constraint first and treat brand work as its own separate project - never as a substitute for fixing what's underneath it.
