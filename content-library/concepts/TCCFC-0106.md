TOPIC:
Founders wait for a co-founder to "just get it" instead of over-communicating vision

HOOK:
Your Co-Founder Isn't a Mind Reader. Stop Expecting Them to Be.

INTRO:
Founders assume shared vision is automatic between co-founders. Point 2 is why that's risky.

POINT 1:
Two people who started a company together don't automatically share an identical picture of where it's going.

POINT 2:
Founders often assume alignment exists because it's never been explicitly tested, not because it's actually been confirmed.

POINT 3:
The founder pairs who stay aligned re-articulate the vision out loud, regularly, instead of assuming it's still shared just because nobody's said otherwise.

CAPTION:
Starting a company together creates a strong feeling of shared vision, and that feeling doesn't automatically stay accurate as the company evolves. Two founders can drift toward meaningfully different pictures of where things are going without ever noticing, because nobody explicitly checked.

Alignment that's never been tested out loud isn't confirmed - it's just assumed, and assumptions about something this important are a risky thing to leave unchecked for months or years.

The founder pairs who stay genuinely aligned re-articulate the vision to each other regularly, even when it feels repetitive, because repetitive is what actually keeps it shared instead of just historically shared.
