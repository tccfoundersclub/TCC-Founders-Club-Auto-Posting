TOPIC:
Assuming your first customers represent your eventual market is a common trap

HOOK:
Your First Ten Customers Might Not Look Anything Like Your Real Market

INTRO:
Early traction feels like proof of who the customer is. Point 2 is why that read can mislead you.

POINT 1:
Early adopters are often unusually forgiving, unusually online, or unusually connected to the founder - not necessarily representative of the broader market.

POINT 2:
Building the roadmap entirely around their specific feedback can optimize for a narrow group instead of the real, larger opportunity.

POINT 3:
The founders who scale well stay alert to the difference between "who found us first" and "who we're actually building for."

CAPTION:
Your first ten or twenty customers feel like proof of who your market is - real people, real usage, real feedback. Often, they're a specific and slightly unusual slice: unusually forgiving of rough edges, unusually online, sometimes connected to the founder directly.

Building the roadmap entirely around their specific requests can end up optimizing for a narrow, atypical group instead of the larger market the business actually needs to reach.

The founders who scale well stay alert to that difference - who found us first isn't automatically the same question as who we're actually building this for.
