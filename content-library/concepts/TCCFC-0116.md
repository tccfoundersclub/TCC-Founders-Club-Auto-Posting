TOPIC:
Founders should audit who they've stopped talking to, not just who they talk to

HOOK:
Look at Who You've Quietly Stopped Talking to. That List Says a Lot Too.

INTRO:
Founders track their active relationships. Point 2 is the equally telling opposite list.

POINT 1:
A network audit usually only looks at current, active relationships - who you talk to, who's in the group chat.

POINT 2:
The people you've quietly drifted from often reveal something too - a relationship that got harder, a connection you let fade without noticing.

POINT 3:
The founders who maintain the strongest networks occasionally revisit the drifted-from list, not just the active one.

CAPTION:
When founders think about their network, they usually think about the active side of it - who they talk to regularly, who's in the group chat, who they'd call for advice. There's a second list that's just as revealing and almost never looked at: who they've quietly stopped talking to.

Some of that drift is natural. Some of it is a relationship that got a little harder, a connection that faded not because it stopped mattering but because nobody made the effort to keep it warm.

The founders who maintain genuinely strong networks occasionally revisit that second list, and reach back out - not out of obligation, but because some of those connections were worth more than the silence suggested.
