TOPIC:
Founders rarely ask their team directly what they'd change

HOOK:
When's the Last Time You Actually Asked Your Team What They'd Change?

INTRO:
Founders assume they'd hear about problems if they existed. Point 2 is why that assumption is weak.

POINT 1:
Waiting for problems to surface on their own relies on people volunteering uncomfortable feedback unprompted, which most people don't do.

POINT 2:
A direct, specific question - "what's one thing you'd change if you could?" - gets answers that silence never will.

POINT 3:
The founders who catch issues early ask this question regularly and directly, instead of assuming an open-door policy is enough.

CAPTION:
A lot of founders believe they'd hear about real problems if they existed - open door policy, approachable, easy to talk to. That belief relies on people volunteering uncomfortable feedback without being asked, which most people simply don't do, even in genuinely open cultures.

A direct, specific question changes that: "what's one thing you'd change if you could?" asked regularly, one-on-one, gets honest answers that an open-door policy alone never will.

The founders who catch issues early don't just make themselves available. They actively ask, on a regular cadence, instead of assuming silence means everything's fine.
