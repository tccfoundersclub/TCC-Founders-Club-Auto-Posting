TOPIC:
"Fake it till you make it" backfires specifically in founder communities built on trust

HOOK:
"Fake It Till You Make It" Works on Strangers. It Doesn't Work in a Real Founder Room.

INTRO:
The advice sounds universally useful. Point 2 is where it actually falls apart.

POINT 1:
Projecting confidence to a customer or investor who doesn't know your full story can genuinely help close a gap.

POINT 2:
In a room of founders who see each other regularly, performed confidence gets noticed and quietly erodes trust over time.

POINT 3:
The founders who build the deepest relationships in a real community are honest about where things actually stand, not performing certainty they don't have.

CAPTION:
"Fake it till you make it" gets repeated as universal startup advice, and it's really only useful in specific, low-context situations - a first pitch, a new customer, someone who doesn't yet know your full story.

In a real founder community, where the same people see each other regularly over months and years, performed confidence doesn't hold up. People notice the gap between what's being said and what's actually happening, and it quietly erodes the trust the room depends on.

The founders who build the deepest relationships in a real community are the honest ones - about the hard month, the thing that isn't working, the doubt. That honesty is what the room is actually for.
