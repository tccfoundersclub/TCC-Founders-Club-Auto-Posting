A "No" From a Customer Six Months Ago Isn't a Permanent No

INTRO:
Founders cross a prospect off the list after one rejection. Point 2 is why that's premature.

POINT 1:
A "not right now" often means exactly that - not right now - and gets filed away as a permanent no instead.

POINT 2:

A prospect says "not right now" and most founders quietly file it away as a permanent no, moving on and never circling back. That's usually a mistake.

"Not right now" often means precisely that - the timing was wrong, not the fit. Budgets open up. Priorities shift. The exact pain point that didn't matter six months ago can suddenly become urgent, and the founder who never followed up misses the moment entirely.

The founders who reopen old conversations respectfully, with a genuine reason to reach back out, close deals a one-and-done approach would have written off completely.
