TOPIC:
Urgency culture ("we need this by Friday") erodes long-term thinking

HOOK:
Everything Being "Urgent by Friday" Is Quietly Killing Your Long-Term Thinking

INTRO:
Constant urgency feels like momentum. Point 2 is what it crowds out.

POINT 1:
A team that's always sprinting toward the next deadline rarely has room left to think about anything past it.

POINT 2:
Manufactured urgency on things that aren't actually urgent trains a team to stop distinguishing real emergencies from routine work.

POINT 3:
The founders who protect strategic thinking are deliberate about which things are genuinely urgent, and let everything else breathe.

CAPTION:
A culture where everything is "urgent, needed by Friday" feels like momentum - fast-moving, high-energy, getting things done. It also quietly crowds out any room for the slower, longer-term thinking that actually shapes where the company goes.

When manufactured urgency gets applied to things that aren't truly urgent, a team eventually stops being able to tell real emergencies apart from routine work. Everything just feels equally frantic, all the time.

The founders who protect real strategic thinking are deliberate about which things actually deserve urgency, and consciously let everything else move at a normal pace.
