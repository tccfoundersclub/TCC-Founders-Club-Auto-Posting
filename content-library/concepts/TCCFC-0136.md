TOPIC:
Founders lose credibility when they take credit for team wins

HOOK:
Taking Credit for a Team Win Costs More Credibility Than It Gains

INTRO:
It feels like good positioning in the moment. Point 2 is the longer cost.

POINT 1:
A founder who quietly absorbs credit for a team's work gets a short-term image boost and a longer-term trust problem.

POINT 2:
The team notices immediately, even when nobody says anything, and it changes how much they're willing to give next time.

POINT 3:
The founders who build the most loyal teams over-credit publicly and let their own contribution go unmentioned more often than feels natural.

CAPTION:
Taking credit for a win the team actually delivered can feel like smart positioning in the moment - to investors, to a room, to a LinkedIn post. It buys a small image boost and costs something bigger: the team notices, every time, even if nobody says anything out loud.

That noticing changes what people are willing to give next time. A team that's watched its work get claimed by someone else holds a little back going forward, quietly, without ever framing it that way.

The founders who build the most loyal teams do the opposite of what feels natural - they over-credit publicly, and let their own role go unmentioned more often than it probably should.
