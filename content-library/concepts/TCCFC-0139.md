TOPIC:
Founders wait for board approval on decisions that don't actually need it

HOOK:
Not Every Decision Needs to Go to the Board

INTRO:
Founders default to seeking approval broadly. Point 2 is what that habit costs in speed.

POINT 1:
Bringing every decision to the board, even ones clearly within the founder's own authority, slows the company down for no real governance benefit.

POINT 2:
Over-consulting the board on small things can also, paradoxically, erode their confidence that the founder can run the company day-to-day.

POINT 3:
The founders who move fast and keep board trust are clear on what actually needs board input, and decide everything else themselves.

CAPTION:
Some founders bring nearly everything to the board - not because governance requires it, but because it feels safer to have shared the decision. The company pays for that safety in speed, on decisions that were clearly within the founder's own authority to just make.

There's also a less obvious cost: constantly bringing small things to the board can quietly erode their confidence that the founder can actually run the company day-to-day without hand-holding.

The founders who move fast and keep strong board trust are clear-eyed about what genuinely needs board input - real strategic or governance decisions - and just make the rest themselves.
