TOPIC:
A founder's online voice not matching their in-person voice creates a trust gap

HOOK:
If Your Online Voice and Your In-Person Voice Don't Match, People Notice

INTRO:
Founders often perform differently online than they are in a room. Point 2 is what that gap costs.

POINT 1:
A founder who's bold and polished online and quiet or uncertain in person creates a small dissonance that people register, even unconsciously.

POINT 2:
That gap doesn't have to be dishonesty - it's often just performance mode versus real mode - but it still reads as inconsistency.

POINT 3:
The founders who build the deepest trust have closed that gap, so who they are online is recognizably the same person in the room.

CAPTION:
A founder can be confident, sharp, and polished online, and noticeably quieter or less certain in person - and people who've met them both ways notice the gap, even if they never say anything about it.

It's rarely dishonesty. It's usually just "performance mode" for content versus how someone actually shows up day to day. Still, that inconsistency registers as something, and it can quietly undermine trust that the online version worked hard to build.

The founders who build the deepest trust have closed that gap - the person online and the person in the room are recognizably the same, even if one version is a little more polished.
