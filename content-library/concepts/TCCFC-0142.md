TOPIC:
Founders personally conducting exit interviews get information nobody else can gather

HOOK:
The Exit Interview You Delegate Is Missing the Only Answer That Actually Matters

INTRO:
Founders often hand off exit interviews. Point 2 is what gets lost in that handoff.

POINT 1:
A departing employee tells HR or a manager a polished, careful version of why they're leaving.

POINT 2:
The same person often tells the founder something different, more honest, if the founder actually asks directly and listens without defensiveness.

POINT 3:
The founders who catch real organizational problems early conduct at least some exit interviews personally, especially for people who mattered.

CAPTION:
Exit interviews often get delegated to HR or a manager, and the departing employee gives a careful, polished version of why they're leaving - true, but softened, aimed at not burning a bridge on the way out.

The same person will often tell the founder something more honest, if the founder actually asks directly, without getting defensive about what comes back. That gap between the polished answer and the real one is exactly where the useful information lives.

The founders who catch real organizational problems early do at least some exit interviews personally, especially for people whose departure actually matters, and treat what they hear as data, not an attack.
