TOPIC:
Founders rarely revisit their own founding assumptions once things are moving

HOOK:
You Probably Haven't Questioned Your Original Assumptions in a Long Time

INTRO:
The founding thesis gets set once and rarely revisited. Point 2 is why that's risky.

POINT 1:
The assumptions a company was founded on made sense at the time, based on the information available then.

POINT 2:
Markets shift, and founding assumptions can quietly stop being true without anyone noticing, because nobody's actively re-testing them.

POINT 3:
The founders who stay ahead of change periodically revisit their original thesis on purpose, treating it as a hypothesis, not settled fact.

CAPTION:
Every company starts with a set of assumptions about the market, the customer, the problem - reasonable, well-considered assumptions at the time. Once the company is moving, those assumptions rarely get revisited. They just quietly become "how things are," whether or not they're still accurate.

Markets shift. What was true at founding can stop being true a year or two later, and nobody notices because nobody's actively checking - the whole team is focused on execution, not re-examining the premise.

The founders who stay ahead of change deliberately revisit their original thesis periodically, treating it as a hypothesis to keep testing, not a settled fact from the past.
