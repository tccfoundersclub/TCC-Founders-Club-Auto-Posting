The word "networking" itself turns some founders off from valuable relationship-building

HOOK:
You Might Be Avoiding "Networking" and Doing the Actual Thing Anyway Without Realizing It

INTRO:
The word carries baggage for a lot of founders. Point 2 is the reframe that helps.

POINT 1:
"Networking" conjures business cards and small talk, and plenty of founders who hate that also happen to be great at real relationships.

POINT 2:
The actual skill that matters - genuine curiosity about people, follow-through, showing up - has nothing to do with the transactional version of the word.

POINT 3:

CAPTION:
A lot of founders genuinely dislike "networking" - the word conjures forced small talk, business cards, transactional energy - and avoid anything labeled that way, even when they're naturally good at building real relationships.

The actual skill that matters has almost nothing to do with the transactional version of the word: genuine curiosity about people, remembering what they told you, following through on what you said you'd do. That's not "networking" in the cringe sense. It's just how good relationships get built.

The founders with the strongest relationships often don't think of themselves as networkers at all. They just care about people and follow through - which turns out to be the whole game.
