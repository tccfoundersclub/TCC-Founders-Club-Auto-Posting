TOPIC:
Treating every setback as a referendum on the whole company distorts perspective

HOOK:
One Bad Week Is Not a Referendum on Whether the Whole Thing Is Working

INTRO:
A rough patch feels like proof of failure. Point 2 is why that read is usually wrong.

POINT 1:
A single bad week gets treated by a lot of founders as evidence about the entire trajectory of the company, which is a huge overreach from one data point.

POINT 2:
That distortion leads to reactive, mood-driven decisions instead of decisions grounded in the actual, longer trend.

POINT 3:
The founders who stay level-headed separate "this week was hard" from "this isn't working," because those are very different statements.

CAPTION:
A single genuinely bad week - a lost deal, a key person quitting, a scary cash moment - can feel like proof that the whole thing isn't working. That's a huge overreach from one data point, and it's an extremely common trap.

Treating a bad week as a verdict on the entire trajectory leads to reactive, mood-driven decisions, made from the emotional low point rather than from the actual longer-term trend, which one week rarely changes much either way.

The founders who stay level-headed through hard stretches keep "this week was hard" and "this isn't working" as two clearly separate statements, and refuse to let one bad week quietly become the second one.
