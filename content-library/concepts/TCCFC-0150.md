TOPIC:
Assuming a great product sells itself is a common and costly founder trap

HOOK:
A Great Product Doesn't Sell Itself. It Just Feels Like It Should.

INTRO:
Founders who build something genuinely good often underinvest in selling it. Point 2 is why that's risky.

POINT 1:
Believing the product is good enough to sell itself quietly justifies under-investing in sales, marketing, and distribution.

POINT 2:
Plenty of genuinely excellent products have failed simply because nobody heard about them at the right moment.

POINT 3:
The founders who succeed with great products still invest seriously in getting them in front of people - quality and distribution aren't substitutes for each other.

CAPTION:
Building something genuinely good creates a quiet, dangerous belief: this is good enough, it'll sell itself, word will spread. That belief usually justifies under-investing in the unglamorous work of actually getting the product in front of people.

The startup graveyard has plenty of genuinely excellent products in it - things that would have worked, if only the right person had heard about them at the right moment. Quality and distribution are not substitutes for each other, no matter how good the product is.

The founders who succeed with great products still take distribution seriously, as its own real discipline, not an afterthought the product's quality was supposed to handle.
