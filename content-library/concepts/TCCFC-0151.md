TOPIC:
Activity in a group chat isn't the same as real community health

HOOK:
A Busy Group Chat Isn't the Same Thing as a Healthy Community

INTRO:
Founders measure community by message volume. Point 2 is why that's the wrong metric.

POINT 1:
A group chat full of messages can be dominated by a handful of loud voices, with most members reading silently and never engaging.

POINT 2:
Real community health looks like depth - people helping each other, real relationships forming - not just volume of activity.

POINT 3:
The founders who build genuinely healthy communities track engagement depth, not just message count, and notice quiet members before they disappear.

CAPTION:
A group chat with constant activity looks healthy from the outside - lots of messages, lots of energy. Often, that volume comes from a handful of loud, active voices, while most members read silently and never actually engage.

Real community health isn't about volume. It's about depth - whether people are actually helping each other, whether real one-on-one relationships are forming out of the group, whether someone new feels like they belong within their first few weeks.

The founders who build genuinely healthy communities pay attention to that depth specifically, and notice the quiet members before they quietly disappear, instead of just watching the message count.
