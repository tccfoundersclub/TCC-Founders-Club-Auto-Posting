TOPIC:
Founders overvalue polished pitch deck design over narrative clarity

HOOK:
A Beautiful Deck With a Confusing Story Still Loses

INTRO:
Design gets the attention. Point 2 is what actually decides the meeting.

POINT 1:
A polished, well-designed deck can make a confusing story feel more finished than it is, without fixing the actual confusion.

POINT 2:
Investors remember whether they understood the business, not whether the slides looked expensive.

POINT 3:
The founders who pitch well nail the narrative first - the logic, the story, the ask - and treat design as the last layer, not the first.

CAPTION:
A lot of founders spend disproportionate time polishing slide design and comparatively little time on whether the underlying story is actually clear. A beautiful deck can make a confusing narrative feel more finished than it is - right up until someone asks a basic question and the story doesn't hold together.

What an investor remembers afterward is whether they understood the business and believed the plan, not whether the slide transitions were smooth.

The founders who pitch well get the narrative right first - the logic, the story arc, the ask - and treat design as the final layer, not the thing doing the heavy lifting.
