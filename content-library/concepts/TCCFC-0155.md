TOPIC:
Founders wait for a mentor's blessing before pivoting

HOOK:
You Don't Need a Mentor's Permission to Pivot

INTRO:
Founders sometimes delay an obvious pivot waiting for outside validation. Point 2 is the cost of that wait.

POINT 1:
Waiting for a mentor to explicitly say "yes, pivot" delays a decision the founder has usually already made internally.

POINT 2:
Mentors give perspective, not authority - the final call on the direction of the company was never actually theirs to make.

POINT 3:
The founders who move decisively use mentor input to sharpen a decision they're going to make either way, not as a gate they need to pass through first.

CAPTION:
A founder who's already sensed the current direction isn't working sometimes still waits - for a mentor's explicit blessing, some outside voice to say "yes, it's okay to pivot" - before actually acting on what they already know.

That wait costs real time. Mentors offer genuinely valuable perspective, and the final call on the company's direction was never actually theirs to make - it's the founder's, whether or not someone else has signed off on it.

The founders who move decisively use mentor conversations to sharpen a decision they're already leaning toward making, not as a gate they need explicit permission to pass through.
