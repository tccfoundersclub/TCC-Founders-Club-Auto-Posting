TOPIC:
Hiring based on resume pedigree alone misses real signal

HOOK:
A Great Resume Tells You Almost Nothing About Whether Someone Can Do This Specific Job

INTRO:
Founders lean on pedigree as a shortcut. Point 2 is why that shortcut misleads.

POINT 1:
A big-name previous employer says something about the hiring bar there, not necessarily about this specific candidate's actual contribution.

POINT 2:
Early-stage startup work requires a different skill set than most polished resumes were built to demonstrate - ambiguity, speed, doing things outside your title.

POINT 3:
The founders who hire well design for the actual job - a real trial project, a specific scenario - instead of leaning on pedigree as a proxy.

CAPTION:
A resume full of impressive names is an easy shortcut in hiring, and it tells you less than it feels like it does. A big-name previous employer reflects that company's hiring bar and team, not necessarily this specific person's actual individual contribution there.

Early-stage startup work demands a different skill set than most resumes were built to demonstrate - comfort with ambiguity, speed, willingness to do things outside a narrow job description. None of that shows up clearly on a polished resume from a polished company.

The founders who hire well design around the actual job - a real trial project, a specific scenario question - instead of leaning on pedigree as a stand-in for genuine signal.
