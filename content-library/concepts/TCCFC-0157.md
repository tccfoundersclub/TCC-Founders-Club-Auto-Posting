TOPIC:
Customer testimonials collected too early are weaker than they seem

HOOK:
The Testimonial You Grabbed in Week One Is Weaker Than It Looks

INTRO:
Early enthusiasm feels like strong social proof. Point 2 is why it often isn't.

POINT 1:
A testimonial from someone's first week of excitement captures enthusiasm, not proven, lasting value.

POINT 2:
The strongest social proof comes from customers who've used the product long enough to have genuinely relied on it, not just tried it.

POINT 3:
The founders who build durable trust with prospects wait for and highlight testimonials from real, sustained use, not just first-impression excitement.

CAPTION:
A glowing testimonial grabbed in someone's first excited week feels like strong social proof - and it mostly captures enthusiasm about a new thing, not evidence of real, lasting value delivered over time.

The strongest testimonials come from customers who've used the product long enough to have genuinely relied on it - who can speak to what changed for them, not just how good the first impression felt.

The founders who build the most durable trust with prospects are patient about this - they collect and highlight testimonials from sustained use, even if it means waiting longer for social proof that actually holds weight.
