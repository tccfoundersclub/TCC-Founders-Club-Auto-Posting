TOPIC:
Founders avoid difficult board members instead of managing the relationship

HOOK:
Avoiding a Difficult Board Member Doesn't Make the Dynamic Better

INTRO:
Founders sometimes go quiet with a challenging board member. Point 2 is why that backfires.

POINT 1:
Minimizing contact with a difficult board member to avoid friction usually makes them more anxious, not less involved.

POINT 2:
Less information from the founder often leads a difficult board member to fill the gap with worse assumptions.

POINT 3:
The founders who manage difficult board relationships well lean in with more communication, not less, even when it's uncomfortable.

CAPTION:
A board member who's harder to work with - more critical, more skeptical, more demanding - sometimes gets quietly avoided: fewer updates, shorter answers, minimal contact to reduce friction. That instinct usually makes the dynamic worse, not better.

Less information from the founder tends to make a difficult board member more anxious, not less involved - and an anxious board member with less information fills the gap with their own, usually worse, assumptions about what's happening.

The founders who manage these relationships well do the counterintuitive thing: more communication, not less, even when the conversations are uncomfortable. Over-informing a skeptical board member is almost always cheaper than under-informing one.
