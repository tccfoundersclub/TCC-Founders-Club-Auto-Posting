TOPIC:
"We don't have a marketing budget" becomes a permanent identity instead of a temporary constraint

HOOK:
"We Don't Have a Marketing Budget" Started as a Fact and Became an Identity

INTRO:
It's true early on. Point 2 is what happens when it stops being examined.

POINT 1:
Having no marketing budget in the early days is a real, temporary constraint that shapes real decisions at the time.

POINT 2:
Left unexamined, that constraint quietly becomes a permanent identity - "we're scrappy, we don't do paid marketing" - long after the constraint has actually lifted.

POINT 3:
The founders who scale efficiently revisit budget constraints regularly, instead of letting an old limitation calcify into a permanent strategy.

CAPTION:
"We don't have a marketing budget" is true and reasonable in the earliest days - a real constraint shaping real decisions. Left unexamined for long enough, it can quietly stop being a temporary fact and become a permanent identity: "we're the scrappy team, we don't really do paid marketing," said long after the actual constraint has lifted.

That identity can end up limiting growth for reasons that no longer exist, simply because nobody revisited whether the original constraint was still true.

The founders who scale efficiently check their old assumptions regularly, and treat "we don't have budget for that" as a fact to re-verify, not a permanent part of who they are.
