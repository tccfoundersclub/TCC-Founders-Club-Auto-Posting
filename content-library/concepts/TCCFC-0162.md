Your Competitor Probably Doesn't Know Something You Don't. They're Guessing Too.

POINT 1:
A competitor's bold, confident move looks like it's based on certainty they have and you don't.

POINT 2:

POINT 3:
The founders who avoid competitor-induced paralysis make decisions from their own data and instincts, instead of reacting to someone else's confident guess.

CAPTION:
A competitor makes a bold, confident move - a big launch, a pricing change, an aggressive expansion - and it's easy to assume they know something you don't, some insight that justified the confidence.

Usually, they're guessing too, under similar uncertainty, and the confidence on display in public is often just good performance rather than proof of a genuinely better read on the market.

The founders who avoid getting paralyzed by watching competitors make decisions rooted in their own data and their own judgment, instead of reacting to someone else's confident guess as if it were certain knowledge.
