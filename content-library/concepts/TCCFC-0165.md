TOPIC:
Founders rarely ask happy customers why they stayed, only unhappy ones why they left

HOOK:
You Interview Customers Who Leave. Do You Ever Ask the Ones Who Stayed Why?

INTRO:
Exit interviews are common practice. Point 2 is the equally useful conversation that's rare.

POINT 1:
Churn interviews get real attention because the loss is visible and urgent. The reasons a customer stayed rarely get the same investigation.

POINT 2:
Understanding why your best customers stayed is often more actionable than understanding why someone left - it tells you what to protect and repeat.

POINT 3:
The founders who grow retention deliberately ask both questions, not just the one that comes with an obvious deadline attached.

CAPTION:
When a customer churns, a lot of teams do the work to understand why - a survey, a call, a real investigation, because the loss is visible and urgent. The mirror question almost never gets the same attention: why did your best, longest-tenured customers actually stay?

Understanding what kept your best customers around is often more directly actionable than understanding why someone left - it tells you exactly what to protect, reinforce, and build more of, rather than just what to avoid.

The founders who grow retention deliberately ask both questions, not just the one that happens to come with an obvious, urgent deadline attached to it.
