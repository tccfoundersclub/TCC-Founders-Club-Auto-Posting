TOPIC:
Founders should maintain one deep expertise area even as they generalize into leadership

HOOK:
Don't Let Leadership Turn You Into a Generalist With No Real Depth Left

INTRO:
Growing into leadership pulls founders away from craft. Point 2 is what's worth protecting.

POINT 1:
As a company grows, a founder's job shifts toward generalist leadership - managing, coordinating, deciding - and away from hands-on craft.

POINT 2:
Losing all hands-on depth can quietly erode a founder's credibility with the specialists actually doing the work, and their own judgment about it.

POINT 3:
The founders who stay sharp protect at least one area of real, current, hands-on depth, even as everything else about the role generalizes.

CAPTION:
As a company grows, a founder's day naturally shifts toward generalist leadership - meetings, coordination, decisions across every function - and away from the specific hands-on craft that maybe started the whole thing.

Losing all hands-on depth entirely has a quiet cost: it becomes harder to evaluate specialist work with real judgment, and harder to earn full credibility with the people doing that work day to day, who can tell when a leader has lost the thread.

The founders who stay sharpest protect at least one area of genuine, current, hands-on expertise, even as the rest of the role inevitably generalizes with the company's growth.
