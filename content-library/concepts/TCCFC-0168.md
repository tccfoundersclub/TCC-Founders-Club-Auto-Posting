TOPIC:
Over-relying on one acquisition channel is a common growth trap

HOOK:
If One Channel Brings in 80% of Your Customers, You Don't Have a Growth Strategy. You Have a Dependency.

INTRO:
A single strong channel feels like a growth engine. Point 2 is the risk hiding in that concentration.

POINT 1:
One dominant channel that's working well feels like a strength, and it's also a single point of failure if that channel's economics or algorithm shifts.

POINT 2:
Founders often don't diversify while the dominant channel is working, because there's no obvious urgency to fix something that isn't broken yet.

POINT 3:
The founders who protect long-term growth start testing a second and third channel while the first one is still strong, not after it suddenly weakens.

CAPTION:
A single acquisition channel bringing in the overwhelming majority of customers feels like a genuine strength - proof you've found what works. It's also a single point of failure: a platform algorithm change, a rising ad cost, a shifting market, and that engine can weaken fast with no backup in place.

Diversifying rarely feels urgent while the dominant channel is still performing well - there's no obvious fire to fight, so the harder, slower work of building a second channel keeps getting deprioritized.

The founders who protect long-term growth start testing additional channels while the first one is still strong, deliberately, rather than scrambling to build one after the first one suddenly weakens.
