TOPIC:
Founders overestimate how unique their problem is when seeking advice

HOOK:
Your Problem Is Probably Not as Unique as It Feels Right Now

INTRO:
Founders often frame their situation as unprecedented. Point 2 is why that framing gets in the way of help.

POINT 1:
"Nobody's dealt with exactly this" feels true in the moment and is usually more about how close you are to it than how rare it is.

POINT 2:
Framing a problem as totally unique makes it harder to accept advice, because the advice-giver's experience feels automatically inapplicable.

POINT 3:
The founders who get the most useful help frame their situation honestly but generically enough that pattern-matching from others can actually work.

CAPTION:
In the middle of a hard problem, it's common to frame it as unprecedented - nobody's dealt with exactly this specific combination of factors. That feeling is mostly about proximity, not actual rarity. Up close, everything looks more unique than it is.

That framing has a real cost: if a problem feels totally unique, advice from someone else's experience gets dismissed as "not quite applicable" before it's even been heard out. The uniqueness becomes a reason to reject help.

The founders who get the most useful advice describe their situation honestly, but generically enough that someone else's pattern-matching actually has room to work. Most problems, stripped of the specific details, are more common than they feel.
