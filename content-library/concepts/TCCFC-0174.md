TOPIC:
Founders don't clarify decision rights with new senior hires, causing early friction

HOOK:
Your New Senior Hire Doesn't Know What They're Actually Allowed to Decide

INTRO:
Founders assume seniority implies clear authority. Point 2 is why that assumption creates friction fast.

POINT 1:
A senior title doesn't automatically communicate which decisions someone can make alone versus which need the founder's sign-off.

POINT 2:
Without that clarity, a new senior hire either oversteps and causes friction, or under-acts and seems less capable than they are.

POINT 3:
The founders who onboard senior hires well spell out decision rights explicitly in the first weeks, instead of letting it get sorted out by trial and error.

CAPTION:
Bringing in a senior hire and assuming the title itself communicates their decision authority is a common gap. It doesn't - a senior title says nothing specific about which calls they can make alone versus which ones still need the founder's sign-off.

Without that clarity spelled out, a new senior hire either oversteps early and causes friction, or plays it too safe and looks less capable and decisive than they actually are - neither outcome reflects who they really are, just an information gap.

The founders who onboard senior hires well have an explicit conversation about decision rights in the first couple of weeks, instead of letting the boundaries get discovered the hard way through trial and error.
