TOPIC:
Chasing press coverage as a proxy for traction is a common trap

HOOK:
A Press Feature Feels Like Traction. It Usually Isn't.

INTRO:
Getting covered feels like validation. Point 2 is why it rarely moves the real numbers.

POINT 1:
A press feature generates a spike of visibility and validation that rarely translates into a lasting change in the actual metrics that matter.

POINT 2:
Founders can spend disproportionate energy chasing coverage because it's exciting and shareable, at the expense of channels that actually convert.

POINT 3:
The founders who grow sustainably treat press as a nice-to-have amplifier of real traction, not a substitute for building it.

CAPTION:
A press feature - a write-up, a podcast mention, a "best startups to watch" list - feels like real traction. It's exciting, shareable, validating. It's also, for most companies, a short spike in visibility that rarely translates into a lasting shift in the metrics that actually matter.

Founders can end up spending disproportionate energy chasing that kind of coverage, precisely because it feels good and is easy to celebrate publicly, at the expense of the slower, less exciting channels that actually convert into customers.

The founders who grow sustainably treat press as a nice amplifier of real, already-existing traction, not a substitute for building that traction in the first place.
