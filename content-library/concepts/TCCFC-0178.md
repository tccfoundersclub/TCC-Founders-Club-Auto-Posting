TOPIC:
Lack of redundancy for key operational knowledge creates a dangerous bus factor

HOOK:
If One Person Left Tomorrow, Would Anyone Know How That Actually Works?

INTRO:
Founders let critical knowledge live in one person's head. Point 2 is why that's a real risk.

POINT 1:
Critical processes often live entirely in one person's head, undocumented, simply because it's faster than writing it down in the moment.

POINT 2:
That concentration feels efficient day-to-day and becomes a genuine crisis the moment that person is unavailable for any reason.

POINT 3:
The founders who protect against this document key processes as they're built, not after someone's already gone and the knowledge left with them.

CAPTION:
A lot of critical operational knowledge in early-stage companies lives entirely in one person's head - how a specific system actually works, who to call when something breaks, the undocumented workaround for a recurring issue. It's faster in the moment to just know it than to write it down.

That concentration feels efficient day-to-day and turns into a real crisis the moment that person is sick, on vacation, or leaves - and nobody else knows how the thing actually works.

The founders who protect against this build documentation into the process as things get built, not as a cleanup project after someone's already gone and taken the knowledge with them.
