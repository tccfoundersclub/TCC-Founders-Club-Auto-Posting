TOPIC:
Treating investors purely as capital misses relationship value

HOOK:
An Investor Who's Just a Line on Your Cap Table Is a Missed Opportunity

INTRO:
Founders sometimes treat investor relationships transactionally. Point 2 is what gets left on the table.

POINT 1:
Investors often bring real value beyond the check - networks, pattern recognition across many companies, specific expertise.

POINT 2:
A founder who only reaches out for money or during a crisis rarely taps into any of that additional value.

POINT 3:
The founders who get the most from their cap table build real relationships with investors, using them as genuine resources, not just as a funding source.

CAPTION:
An investor relationship can end up reduced to a name on the cap table and a monthly update email - purely transactional, activated only when more money or a crisis makes contact necessary. That leaves real value on the table.

Investors often bring far more than capital: networks built across many companies, pattern recognition about what tends to work and what doesn't, specific expertise a founder might not have access to anywhere else. A purely transactional relationship rarely taps into any of that.

The founders who get the most from their investor base treat them as genuine resources - reaching out with real questions, not just updates, and building relationships that go beyond the check.
