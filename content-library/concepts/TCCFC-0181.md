TOPIC:
"Ask forgiveness, not permission" backfires in certain founder relationships

HOOK:
"Ask Forgiveness, Not Permission" Is Good Advice for Some Situations and Terrible for Others

INTRO:
The startup mantra gets applied universally. Point 2 is where it actually damages trust.

POINT 1:
The mantra works well for low-stakes, reversible decisions where speed genuinely matters more than consensus.

POINT 2:
Applied to relationships built on trust - co-founders, key investors, close team members - it can feel like a betrayal of the relationship itself.

POINT 3:
The founders who use this principle well are deliberate about where it applies, instead of treating it as a universal operating philosophy.

CAPTION:
"Ask forgiveness, not permission" gets repeated as a universal startup principle, and it's really only good advice in specific situations - low-stakes, reversible decisions where speed genuinely matters more than getting buy-in first.

Applied to relationships built on real trust - a co-founder, a key investor, a close team member - the same principle can feel like a quiet betrayal of that trust, especially if it's used repeatedly on decisions that actually affected them.

The founders who use this principle well are deliberate about exactly where it applies, and treat trust-based relationships as needing a different standard than fast, reversible operational calls.
