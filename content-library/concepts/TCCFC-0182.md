TOPIC:
Founders over-promising in sales to hit a number costs more later

HOOK:
The Deal You Closed by Over-Promising Is Going to Cost You More Than It Made

INTRO:
Hitting the number feels urgent in the moment. Point 2 is the bill that comes due later.

POINT 1:
Stretching the truth about a timeline or a feature to close a deal solves the immediate pressure and creates a debt that has to be paid later.

POINT 2:
That debt shows up as a frustrated customer, an emergency engineering sprint, or a damaged relationship - almost always more expensive than the deal was worth.

POINT 3:
The founders who sell sustainably say no to a deal that requires over-promising, even when the number is tempting in the moment.

CAPTION:
Stretching the truth a little in a sales conversation - a timeline that isn't quite real, a feature that isn't quite ready - can close a deal and relieve real pressure in the moment. It also creates a debt that comes due later, usually with interest.

That debt shows up as a frustrated customer discovering the gap, an emergency sprint to build something that was promised too early, or a damaged relationship that costs more in trust than the deal was ever worth in revenue.

The founders who sell sustainably say no to closing a deal that requires over-promising, even when the number in front of them is genuinely tempting in the moment.
