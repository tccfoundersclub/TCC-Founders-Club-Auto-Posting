TOPIC:
Founders avoid necessary price increases on existing customers out of fear

HOOK:
You're Probably Overdue for a Price Increase and Avoiding It Out of Fear, Not Strategy

INTRO:
Raising prices on existing customers feels riskier than it usually is. Point 2 is the real math.

POINT 1:
Fear of losing existing customers to a price increase is usually bigger than the actual churn risk, especially for a product delivering real value.

POINT 2:
Avoiding a needed increase for years compounds into a much bigger, more painful jump later, instead of smaller regular adjustments.

POINT 3:
The founders who price confidently treat regular increases as normal business practice, communicated clearly, not as a rare, high-stakes event to dread.

CAPTION:
The fear of losing existing customers to a price increase almost always looms larger than the actual risk, especially for a product that's genuinely delivering value - most customers who are getting real value tolerate a reasonable, well-communicated increase better than founders expect.

Avoiding a needed increase for one year, then another, compounds into a much bigger and more painful jump eventually, instead of smaller, more digestible adjustments made along the way as costs and value both increase.

The founders who price with confidence treat regular increases as a normal part of running a business - clearly communicated, reasonably justified - rather than a rare, dreaded event they keep putting off.
