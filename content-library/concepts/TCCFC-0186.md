TOPIC:
Assuming remote hires need less onboarding investment is a costly mistake

HOOK:
Remote Hires Need More Onboarding Investment, Not Less

INTRO:
Founders sometimes assume remote hiring simplifies onboarding. Point 2 is why that assumption backfires.

POINT 1:
In-office hires absorb a lot of context passively - overheard conversations, casual introductions, ambient culture. Remote hires get none of that by default.

POINT 2:
Assuming a remote hire will "figure it out" the way an in-office hire naturally does leaves them isolated and slower to ramp.

POINT 3:
The founders who onboard remote hires well build deliberate structure to replace what used to happen accidentally in an office.

CAPTION:
An in-office hire absorbs a lot of context passively just by being physically present - overheard conversations, casual hallway introductions, a general feel for the culture that nobody had to explicitly teach. A remote hire gets none of that unless someone builds it deliberately.

Assuming a remote hire will "figure it out" the same way, simply because onboarding logistics feel simpler without an office to manage, leaves them isolated, slower to build relationships, and slower to actually ramp up.

The founders who onboard remote hires well build deliberate structure specifically to replace what used to happen by accident in a physical office - intentional introductions, more frequent early check-ins, explicit context that used to be ambient.
