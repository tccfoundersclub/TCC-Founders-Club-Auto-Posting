CAPTION:
A crisis that almost happened - caught just in time, resolved partly through luck rather than a solid system - rarely gets the same scrutiny as an actual failure. There's a natural relief that overshadows the impulse to dig into why it almost went wrong in the first place.

Near-misses often reveal exactly the same underlying weakness a real failure would have exposed, minus the painful consequence that would have forced the conversation to happen.

The founders who build genuinely resilient systems treat a near-miss as a free lesson - the same information a failure would have given, at none of the cost - and investigate it with just as much seriousness.
