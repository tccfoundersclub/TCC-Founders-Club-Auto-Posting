TOPIC:
The first customer complaint about a new feature isn't proof it was the wrong call

HOOK:
The First Complaint About a New Feature Doesn't Mean You Built the Wrong Thing

INTRO:
Early negative feedback feels like a verdict. Point 2 is why it's usually premature.

POINT 1:
A vocal early complaint often comes from someone used to the old way, reacting to change itself, not necessarily to the change being wrong.

POINT 2:
Reversing a decision based on the first loud reaction can throw away something that would have proven right with a little more time and iteration.

POINT 3:
The founders who ship real improvements distinguish between "this is different and uncomfortable" and "this is actually worse," and give real change enough time to be judged fairly.

CAPTION:
A new feature ships and the first reaction is a complaint, sometimes a loud one. It's tempting to read that as proof the change was a mistake. Often, that first complaint comes from someone reacting to change itself - the discomfort of something being different - more than to the change actually being worse.

Reversing course based on the first loud reaction can throw away something that would have proven genuinely better with a little more time, a little more iteration, and a little more patience from everyone involved, including the founder.

The founders who ship real, lasting improvements learn to distinguish between "this feels different and uncomfortable" and "this is actually worse," and give real change enough runway to be judged fairly before reacting to the loudest first response.
