TOPIC:
Hiring a "rockstar" without checking how they treat junior people is a common blind spot

HOOK:
Before You Hire the "Rockstar," Watch How They Treat the Most Junior Person in the Room

INTRO:
Impressive individual performers get evaluated on output alone. Point 2 is the signal that matters just as much.

POINT 1:
A candidate's brilliance in an interview says nothing about how they'll treat people with less power or experience once they're actually on the team.

POINT 2:
How someone treats a junior colleague or a support person is a strong predictor of team culture impact, far more than their resume or their interview polish.

POINT 3:
The founders who protect team culture make this an explicit part of the hiring process, not something they hope works out after the fact.

CAPTION:
A candidate who's clearly brilliant and impressive in an interview says a lot about their individual capability and almost nothing about how they'll treat people with less power once they're actually on the team - a junior hire, a support person, someone whose role isn't as visible.

That specific behavior - warmth or dismissiveness toward people who can't do much for their career - is a far stronger predictor of real culture impact than polish in an interview room built to showcase their best self.

The founders who protect their culture check this deliberately, as part of the actual hiring process - a reference check that asks this specific question, an interaction observed - not something they simply hope works out once the person's already hired.
