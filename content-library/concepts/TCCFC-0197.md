TOPIC:
Inconsistent decision-making speed (sometimes fast, sometimes slow, unpredictably) confuses teams

HOOK:
Your Team Can't Predict Whether You'll Decide in Five Minutes or Five Weeks, and That's a Problem

INTRO:
Founders vary decision speed based on mood or bandwidth. Point 2 is what that unpredictability costs.

POINT 1:
Decision speed that swings unpredictably - fast on some things, glacially slow on others, with no visible pattern - makes it hard for a team to plan around the founder.

POINT 2:
Without a predictable rhythm, people either over-escalate small things (afraid of a slow response) or under-escalate big things (assuming it'll take forever anyway).

POINT 3:
The founders who are easiest to work with are predictable about decision speed, even if the actual speed varies by category, because the team can plan around a known pattern.

CAPTION:
A founder who decides some things in five minutes and other, seemingly similar things over five weeks - with no visible pattern to when which happens - creates real uncertainty for the team trying to plan around them.

Without a predictable rhythm, people start managing around the unpredictability itself: over-escalating small decisions out of fear of a slow response, or under-escalating genuinely important ones because "that'll probably take forever anyway." Neither is the outcome anyone wants.

The founders who are easiest to work with aren't necessarily the fastest deciders - they're the predictable ones. The team knows roughly what kind of decision gets what kind of response time, and can plan accordingly.
