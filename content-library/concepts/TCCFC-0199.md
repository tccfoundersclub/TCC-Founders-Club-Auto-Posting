TOPIC:
Assuming a satisfied customer will renew automatically is a costly assumption

HOOK:
A Satisfied Customer Isn't the Same as a Customer Who's Going to Renew

INTRO:
Founders equate satisfaction with retention. Point 2 is why that link is weaker than it seems.

POINT 1:
A customer can be genuinely satisfied and still not renew, simply because the renewal conversation never happened proactively.

POINT 2:
Satisfaction measures how someone feels about the product. Renewal is a decision that needs an actual prompt and a clear reason to act.

POINT 3:
The founders who protect retention treat renewal as something to actively manage, not something satisfaction automatically produces on its own.

CAPTION:
A customer being genuinely satisfied with the product feels like it should guarantee renewal, and it doesn't automatically - satisfaction measures how someone feels, renewal is an active decision that needs its own prompt, timing, and reason to act.

Plenty of satisfied customers quietly lapse simply because nobody proactively managed the renewal conversation - no reminder, no check-in, no clear next step, just an assumption that a happy customer will handle it themselves.

The founders who protect retention treat renewal as something to actively manage - a real process, not a passive outcome that satisfaction is assumed to produce on its own.
