Founders don't clarify what happens if they're unreachable for a week

If You Disappeared for a Week With No Warning, Would Your Team Know What to Do?

INTRO:
Founders rarely plan for their own unavailability. Point 2 is the risk that creates.

POINT 1:
A founder is often the default decision-maker for anything ambiguous, and there's rarely an explicit plan for what happens if they're simply unreachable.

POINT 2:
Without that plan, a founder's sudden unavailability - illness, an emergency, anything - can genuinely stall the business at exactly the wrong moment.

POINT 3:
The founders who build resilient companies define, in advance, who decides what when they're unreachable, instead of leaving it to be improvised under pressure.

CAPTION:
A founder is usually the default decision-maker for anything genuinely ambiguous, and there's rarely an explicit plan for what happens if they're simply unreachable for a stretch - an illness, a family emergency, anything unplanned and real.

Without that plan, a founder's sudden unavailability can genuinely stall parts of the business at exactly the moment it can least afford to stall, because nobody was ever explicitly told what they're allowed to decide without waiting for the founder's return.

The founders who build genuinely resilient companies define this in advance - who has authority over what, in writing, for exactly this scenario - instead of leaving it to be figured out under pressure during an actual emergency.
