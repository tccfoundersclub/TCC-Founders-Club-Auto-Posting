TOPIC:
Treating every hire as permanent from day one creates hesitation to hire at all

HOOK:
Treating Every Hire Like It Has to Be Permanent Is Making You Hire Too Slowly

INTRO:
The weight of "forever" makes hiring decisions feel enormous. Point 2 is why that framing slows growth.

POINT 1:
Framing every hire as a permanent, high-stakes commitment makes the decision feel enormous, and enormous decisions get delayed.

POINT 2:
Most hires aren't actually permanent - people grow, roles evolve, and a hire that's wrong for one stage can still be right for the moment it was made in.

POINT 3:
The founders who hire at a healthy pace treat hiring as an important but reversible decision, not a permanent one, which makes acting on it faster.

CAPTION:
Framing every hire as a permanent, forever commitment makes the decision feel enormous - and enormous decisions naturally get delayed, second-guessed, and pushed back while a role sits open longer than it needs to.

Most hires aren't actually permanent in practice. People grow into or out of roles, priorities shift, and a hire that's right for this specific stage of the company doesn't have to be evaluated as if it needs to last forever to be worth making.

The founders who hire at a healthy pace treat hiring as an important, real decision that's still reversible if needed - not a permanent one - which makes it much easier to actually act on when a role genuinely needs filling.
