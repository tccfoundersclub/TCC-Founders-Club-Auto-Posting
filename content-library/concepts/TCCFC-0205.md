TOPIC:
Founders benefit from having a peer who tells them to slow down, not just speed up

HOOK:
Everyone in Your Circle Tells You to Move Faster. Who Tells You to Slow Down?

INTRO:
Founder culture rewards speed almost universally. Point 2 is the rarer, equally valuable voice.

POINT 1:
Most founder peer groups reinforce urgency - move faster, ship sooner, raise bigger - because that's the dominant, celebrated instinct in the culture.

POINT 2:
A voice that occasionally says "you don't actually need to rush this one" is rare, and often exactly right when it shows up.

POINT 3:
The founders who avoid burnout and rushed mistakes keep at least one relationship that gives them permission to slow down, not just pressure to speed up.

CAPTION:
Most founder peer groups reinforce the same instinct almost universally: move faster, ship sooner, raise bigger, do more. That energy is genuinely useful and it's also one-directional - almost nobody in the room is pushing the opposite case.

A voice that occasionally says "you don't actually need to rush this specific decision" is rare precisely because it goes against the dominant culture, and it's often exactly the perspective that prevents a genuinely costly, rushed mistake.

The founders who avoid burnout and avoidable errors keep at least one relationship in their life that gives them permission to slow down when it's warranted, not just pressure to speed up all the time.
