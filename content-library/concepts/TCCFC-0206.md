TOPIC:
Founders confuse a quiet customer base with a happy one

HOOK:
A Quiet Customer Base Isn't the Same Thing as a Happy One

INTRO:
Low complaint volume feels reassuring. Point 2 is why it can mislead.

POINT 1:
Few complaints can mean genuine satisfaction, and it can also mean customers have simply stopped bothering to engage or complain at all.

POINT 2:
Disengagement is quieter than complaint, and it's often the more dangerous signal, because it precedes churn with no warning.

POINT 3:

CAPTION:
A quiet customer base - few complaints, few support tickets, not much noise either way - can feel reassuring, like everything's fine. Sometimes it means genuine satisfaction. Sometimes it means customers have simply stopped engaging enough to bother complaining at all.

Disengagement is a quieter signal than complaint, and it's often the more dangerous one - it tends to precede churn with almost no warning, because nobody ever said anything was wrong before they just quietly left.

The founders who read their customer base accurately actively check real engagement levels, not just complaint volume, specifically to tell genuine satisfaction apart from silent disengagement before it becomes churn.
