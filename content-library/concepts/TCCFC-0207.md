TOPIC:
Chasing a competitor's feature instead of your own roadmap logic is a common trap

HOOK:
Just Because a Competitor Shipped It Doesn't Mean It Belongs on Your Roadmap

INTRO:
A competitor's move creates pressure to match it. Point 2 is why matching isn't always the right call.

POINT 1:
A competitor shipping a new feature creates real pressure to respond, whether or not that feature actually fits your own product's logic.

POINT 2:
Chasing every competitor move turns a roadmap into a reactive list instead of a coherent plan built around your own customers' actual needs.

POINT 3:
The founders who build coherent products evaluate a competitor's move against their own strategy first, and only add it if it genuinely fits.

CAPTION:
A competitor ships a new feature and there's real pressure to respond - to not look like you're falling behind, to give your team something similar to point to. That pressure exists whether or not the feature actually fits your own product's logic or your own customers' needs.

Chasing every competitor move turns a roadmap into a reactive list of "things they have that we don't," instead of a coherent plan built around your own strategy and your own understanding of what your customers actually need.

The founders who build genuinely coherent products evaluate a competitor's move against their own roadmap logic first, and only add it if it actually fits - not simply because it exists somewhere else.
