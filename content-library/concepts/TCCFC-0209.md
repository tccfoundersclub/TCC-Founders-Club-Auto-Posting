TOPIC:
Founders lack a plan for what happens to customers if the company shuts down

HOOK:
If Your Company Shut Down Tomorrow, What Happens to Your Customers' Data and Money?

INTRO:
Founders rarely plan for the worst case explicitly. Point 2 is why that gap matters, even if it never happens.

POINT 1:
Almost no early-stage company has an explicit plan for what happens to customer data, subscriptions, or funds in a shutdown scenario.

POINT 2:
Not having that plan doesn't just create legal risk - it's also a real ethical gap, since customers trusted you with something real.

POINT 3:
The founders who build real trust think this through in advance, even briefly, instead of assuming it'll never actually be needed.

CAPTION:
Almost no early-stage company has an explicit answer for what happens to customer data, active subscriptions, or held funds in a genuine shutdown scenario - it's an uncomfortable thing to plan for while everything is still going well, so it rarely gets planned for at all.

That gap isn't just a legal exposure, though it can be that too. It's also a real ethical one: customers trusted a company with something - their data, their money, their workflow - and deserve to know there's at least a basic plan for what happens to it in the worst case.

The founders who build the deepest trust think this through in advance, even briefly, rather than assuming it's a scenario so unlikely it doesn't need a real answer.
