TOPIC:
Tracking what almost went wrong in a launch reveals more than tracking only what did

HOOK:
What Almost Broke During Your Last Launch? Nobody Usually Writes That Down.

INTRO:
Post-launch reviews focus on what actually happened. Point 2 is the equally valuable question that gets skipped.

POINT 1:
A launch retro typically covers what went wrong and what went right, and rarely covers what came close to going wrong but didn't, by luck or last-minute save.

POINT 2:
Those near-misses reveal the same underlying fragility a real failure would have exposed, without the painful consequence that would force the conversation.

POINT 3:
The founders who build more resilient launch processes ask specifically what almost broke, not just what actually did.

CAPTION:
A launch retrospective typically covers what actually went wrong and what actually went right - a reasonable, standard review. It rarely covers the third category: what almost broke, caught at the last minute, saved by luck or a frantic fix nobody talks about afterward.

Those near-misses reveal exactly the same underlying fragility a real failure would have exposed, minus the painful consequence that would have forced the team to take it seriously.

The founders who build genuinely more resilient launch processes ask specifically about what almost went wrong, not just what actually did, because that's where the free lessons are hiding.
