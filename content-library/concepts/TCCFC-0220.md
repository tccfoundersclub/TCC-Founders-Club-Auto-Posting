TOPIC:
Founders assume growth will fix culture problems

HOOK:
Growth Doesn't Fix a Culture Problem. It Usually Just Makes It Bigger.

INTRO:
Founders sometimes hope scale will smooth over existing issues. Point 2 is why that hope backfires.

POINT 1:
A culture issue at ten people - unclear roles, avoided conflict, unequal treatment - doesn't resolve itself as the company grows; it just gets more people caught in it.

POINT 2:
Founders sometimes deprioritize addressing an early culture problem, hoping new hires and momentum will dilute it naturally.

POINT 3:
The founders who scale well fix culture problems at the size they're at, because the same problem at twice the headcount is twice as expensive to fix.

CAPTION:
A real culture issue at ten people - unclear roles, conflict nobody addresses, unequal treatment between team members - doesn't resolve itself as the company scales. It just recruits more people into the same broken pattern, at a larger and more expensive scale.

It's tempting to deprioritize an early culture problem, hoping growth and new hires will naturally dilute or outgrow it. That hope is usually misplaced - patterns that go unaddressed tend to get reinforced by momentum, not corrected by it.

The founders who scale smoothly fix culture problems at whatever size they currently are, because the exact same problem at twice the headcount is measurably harder and more expensive to unwind later.
