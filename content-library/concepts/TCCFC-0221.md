TOPIC:
Hiring two co-founders to "cover more bases" without clarity creates its own risk

HOOK:
Adding a Second Co-Founder to "Cover More Bases" Without Clear Roles Just Adds a Second Set of Blind Spots

INTRO:
More co-founders feels like more coverage. Point 2 is the risk that framing misses.

POINT 1:
Bringing on an additional co-founder specifically to fill skill gaps can work well, and it needs the same role clarity any hire would need - often more.

POINT 2:
Without clearly defined ownership between three or more co-founders, decision-making gets slower and disagreements get harder to resolve, not easier.

POINT 3:
The founders who add co-founders successfully define decision rights explicitly from day one, treating clarity as more important than simply adding coverage.

CAPTION:
Bringing on an additional co-founder to cover skills the existing team is missing can genuinely work well - and it needs exactly the same role clarity any critical hire would need, arguably more, because co-founder equity and authority are much harder to unwind later than an employee's.

Without clearly defined ownership between three or more co-founders, decisions don't get easier with more people in the room - they often get slower, and disagreements get genuinely harder to resolve when there's no clear tiebreaker.

The founders who add co-founders successfully define decision rights explicitly from the very first conversation, treating that clarity as more important than simply adding more coverage to the team.
