TOPIC:
"We're focused on product, not marketing" becomes a permanent excuse

HOOK:
"We're a Product-Focused Company, Not a Marketing One" Can Quietly Become a Permanent Excuse

INTRO:
The framing sounds principled. Point 2 is how it can go too far.

POINT 1:
Prioritizing product over marketing early on is often the right call, given limited resources and an unproven idea.

POINT 2:
Left unexamined, "we're product-focused" can calcify into an identity that justifies permanently underinvesting in getting the product in front of people.

POINT 3:
The founders who scale well revisit this balance as the company matures, instead of treating an early prioritization as a permanent philosophy.

CAPTION:
Prioritizing product development over marketing spend is often genuinely the right call early on, when resources are limited and the idea itself is still unproven - build something worth marketing before spending heavily to market it.

Left unexamined for too long, "we're a product-focused company" can quietly calcify from a reasonable early prioritization into a permanent identity - one that justifies indefinitely underinvesting in the work of getting a genuinely good product in front of the people who'd actually want it.

The founders who scale well revisit this balance as the company matures, treating the early prioritization as a stage-appropriate choice, not a permanent philosophy that never gets re-examined.
