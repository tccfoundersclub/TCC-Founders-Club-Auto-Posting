TOPIC:
Founders should keep a swipe file of customer language, not just internal jargon

HOOK:
Your Customers Describe Their Own Problem Better Than Your Team Does. Are You Writing That Down?

INTRO:
Internal teams develop their own vocabulary for the product. Point 2 is what gets lost in that shift.

POINT 1:
Internal teams naturally develop jargon and shorthand that drifts away from how real customers actually talk about their own problem.

POINT 2:
Customers' own words - in support tickets, reviews, sales calls - are often sharper and more resonant marketing copy than anything a team writes internally.

POINT 3:
The founders who write the most resonant marketing keep an active swipe file of actual customer language, and use it directly instead of paraphrasing.

CAPTION:
Internal teams naturally develop their own shorthand and jargon for the product over time, and that language quietly drifts away from how real customers actually describe their own problem in their own words.

Customers' actual language - in support tickets, in reviews, on sales calls, in casual conversation - is often sharper, more specific, and more resonant than anything a marketing team writes internally, because it's rooted in a real, lived frustration rather than a product description.

The founders who write the most resonant marketing keep an active, growing swipe file of real customer language and use it close to verbatim, instead of paraphrasing it into something that sounds more "professional" and less true.
