CAPTION:
Someone who's given real, consistent, genuinely valuable input over many months - a former colleague, an experienced friend, a connection who keeps showing up with good advice - often never gets formally recognized as an advisor. They're just quietly leaned on, informally, indefinitely.

Without some formal recognition - a small equity grant, an explicit title, a clear acknowledgment of the relationship - that arrangement can drift. The advisor's sense of investment can fade, and the value they were providing can quietly taper off without anyone naming why.

The founders who protect genuinely valuable informal relationships formalize them once the value is clearly proven, instead of assuming an unstructured arrangement will simply continue indefinitely on its own.
