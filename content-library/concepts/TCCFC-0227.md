TOPIC:
Assuming your loudest customer represents your average customer is a common trap

HOOK:
Your Loudest Customer Is Not Your Average Customer, and Treating Them Like One Skews Your Roadmap

INTRO:
The most vocal customer gets disproportionate attention. Point 2 is why that skews decisions.

POINT 1:
A customer who emails frequently, posts publicly, or shows up in every survey shapes a founder's sense of "what customers want" disproportionately.

POINT 2:
The vast majority of customers never say anything at all, and their actual needs can look quite different from the loudest voice's specific requests.

POINT 3:
The founders who build for the real market actively seek out the silent majority's input, instead of letting the loudest customer set the agenda by default.

CAPTION:
A customer who emails frequently, comments publicly, and shows up in every survey ends up shaping a founder's mental model of "what our customers want" far more than their actual proportion of the customer base would justify.

The vast majority of customers never say anything at all - not because they're unhappy, often just because most people don't proactively give feedback - and their real needs and preferences can look meaningfully different from whatever the loudest, most vocal customer keeps asking for.

The founders who build for the real, full market actively go looking for the silent majority's input, through surveys, direct outreach, actual usage data, instead of letting the loudest customer set the roadmap agenda by default.
