CAPTION:
The specific reasoning behind a pivot feels completely vivid and obvious in the moment it's decided - of course we remember why we did this. That clarity fades faster than most founders expect, especially once attention has moved fully onto the new direction.

Months or years later, facing a new hard decision that echoes the old one, the original "why" gets reconstructed from memory - often inaccurately, shaped by how things turned out rather than what was actually known and reasoned through at the time.

The founders who make consistent strategic decisions over time write down the actual reasoning behind major pivots as they happen, so future-them has real data to work from instead of a fuzzy, retroactively-edited memory.
