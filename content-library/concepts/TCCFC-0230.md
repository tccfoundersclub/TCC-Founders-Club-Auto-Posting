TOPIC:
Founders treat their first hire's opinion as gospel long after it should still hold that weight

HOOK:
Your First Hire's Opinion Earned Enormous Trust Two Years Ago. Does It Still Deserve the Same Weight Today?

INTRO:
Early trust calcifies. Point 2 is why it's worth periodically re-examining.

POINT 1:
A first or early hire who was right about a lot in the beginning earns enormous, well-deserved trust and influence.

POINT 2:
That trust can calcify into treating their opinion as automatically correct on everything, long after the company and its needs have evolved past their original context.

POINT 3:
The founders who make the best decisions re-evaluate influence periodically based on current judgment, not just historical track record.

CAPTION:
An early hire who was right about a lot of important things in the company's first year earns real, well-deserved trust and influence - they helped build something real, often under genuinely hard conditions.

That trust can quietly calcify into treating their opinion as automatically correct on everything indefinitely, long after the company, the market, and the actual problems being solved have evolved well past the context that original judgment was built in.

The founders who make the best decisions re-evaluate influence periodically based on current judgment and current context, not purely on a historical track record that may no longer map cleanly onto today's actual decisions.
