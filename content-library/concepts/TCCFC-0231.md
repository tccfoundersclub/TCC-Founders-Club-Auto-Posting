A co-founder's departure, for any reason - conflict, burnout, a mutual decision to part ways - is one of the most destabilizing events a young company can go through, and it's almost never planned for in advance, because it feels premature or even bad luck to consider it.

Without any real communication plan, the actual announcement to the team, to investors, to customers often gets improvised under real emotional pressure, at exactly the moment improvisation is least reliable.

The founders who navigate this well, even if it's just a hypothetical conversation had once, in calmer times, handle the actual moment with far more stability and clarity than those caught completely unprepared by something they never let themselves think about.
