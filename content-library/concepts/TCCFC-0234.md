TOPIC:
Founders assume a customer's silence after onboarding means success

HOOK:
A New Customer Who Goes Quiet After Onboarding Might Not Be a Success Story

INTRO:
Founders read post-onboarding silence as a good sign. Point 2 is why that read can be wrong.

POINT 1:
No questions and no complaints after onboarding can mean the product just clicked, or it can mean the customer quietly gave up trying to use it.

POINT 2:
Those two outcomes look identical from the outside - silence - and lead to very different eventual results if nobody checks.

POINT 3:
The founders who catch early churn risk proactively check in after onboarding instead of treating silence as automatic confirmation of success.

CAPTION:
A new customer who goes quiet right after onboarding - no follow-up questions, no complaints, nothing - can mean the product just clicked and they're happily using it on their own. It can also mean they hit friction, quietly gave up, and never bothered to say anything before drifting away.

Those two very different outcomes look completely identical from the outside: silence. Without actually checking in, there's no way to know which one actually happened until it shows up later as a renewal that doesn't happen.

The founders who catch this kind of early risk proactively reach out after onboarding, rather than treating silence as automatic confirmation that everything worked.
