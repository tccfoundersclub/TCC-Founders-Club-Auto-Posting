TOPIC:
Founders rarely examine whether their meetings actually need the founder present

HOOK:
Half the Meetings on Your Calendar Probably Don't Actually Need You There

INTRO:
Founders default to attending broadly. Point 2 is what that habit costs the team.

POINT 1:
A founder's default presence in meetings, "just in case," can subtly stop the team from developing the confidence to decide things without them.

POINT 2:
Meetings that don't strictly need the founder still often include them, simply out of habit or unexamined assumption.

POINT 3:
The founders who build real team autonomy audit their meeting attendance and deliberately step back from ones the team can genuinely run alone.

CAPTION:
A founder's habitual presence in meetings "just in case something needs a decision" feels responsible, and it can subtly stop a team from developing the confidence and practice to make those decisions without the founder in the room.

A lot of meetings that don't strictly require the founder's input still include them, simply out of habit, or because nobody ever questioned whether the attendance was actually necessary in the first place.

The founders who build genuine team autonomy periodically audit their own meeting attendance and deliberately step back from the ones the team is fully capable of running well alone.
