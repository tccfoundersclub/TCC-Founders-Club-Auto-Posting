TOPIC:
Founders rarely revisit whether their original co-founder equity split still feels fair

HOOK:
The Equity Split You Agreed to on Day One Might Not Reflect Who's Actually Building the Company Now

INTRO:
Founders treat the original split as permanently settled. Point 2 is why that can create quiet resentment.

POINT 1:
An equity split decided on day one reflected the plan and contributions expected at that moment, which can shift significantly as the company evolves.

POINT 2:
If one co-founder's actual contribution grows disproportionately without any corresponding conversation about equity, quiet resentment can build on both sides.

POINT 3:
The founders who avoid this friction have an explicit, if rare, conversation about whether the original split still reflects reality as things change.

CAPTION:
An equity split decided on day one reflected a plan and a set of expected contributions at that specific moment - reasonable at the time, and not necessarily still accurate a few years in, once the company and each person's actual role has evolved.

If one co-founder's real contribution has grown well beyond what the original split anticipated, without any conversation ever happening about it, quiet resentment can build on both sides - one feeling under-recognized, the other unaware anything feels off.

The founders who avoid this kind of friction have an explicit, if genuinely uncomfortable, conversation periodically about whether the original split still reflects current reality, rather than treating day-one numbers as permanently untouchable.
