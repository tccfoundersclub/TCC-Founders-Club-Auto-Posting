Most founders have a private, honest sense of their own biggest weakness as a leader - maybe it's follow-through, maybe it's patience, maybe it's giving direct feedback. That awareness usually stays private, never said out loud to the people who actually experience the weakness firsthand.

Naming it explicitly to the team changes something real: it gives everyone permission to work around it constructively and directly, instead of quietly, individually managing around an unnamed pattern that nobody's allowed to acknowledge.

The founders who build the most effective teams are specific and public about their own real gaps, and actively ask the team to help cover for them - which usually earns more respect, not less.
