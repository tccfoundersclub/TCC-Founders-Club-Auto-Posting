TOPIC:
Founders should distinguish between customers who complain to help versus customers who complain to vent

HOOK:
Not Every Complaint Is Asking You to Fix Something

INTRO:
All complaints get treated as actionable feedback. Point 2 is why that's not quite right.

POINT 1:
Some complaints come with an implicit request for a fix. Others are simply someone venting frustration, without necessarily wanting the founder to overhaul anything.

POINT 2:
Treating every complaint as an urgent roadmap item can lead to reactive, thrashing product decisions driven by whoever vented loudest recently.

POINT 3:
The founders who use feedback well learn to distinguish tone and intent, and respond to venting with empathy rather than an immediate feature commitment.

CAPTION:
Some customer complaints come with an implicit request for a fix - a real, specific problem they want resolved. Others are simply someone venting frustration in the moment, without necessarily expecting or even wanting the founder to overhaul anything based on it.

Treating every complaint as an urgent, actionable roadmap item can lead to reactive, thrashing product decisions - chasing whoever vented loudest most recently, rather than following a coherent strategy.

The founders who use feedback well learn to distinguish tone and actual intent, responding to genuine venting with empathy and a listening ear, and reserving real roadmap commitments for feedback that's actually asking for a fix.
