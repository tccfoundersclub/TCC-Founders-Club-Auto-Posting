A newer founder asks for help, and there's sometimes a small, quiet hesitation underneath the willingness to give it - an unexamined scarcity instinct that there's only so much opportunity, attention, and success to go around, and helping someone else somehow uses up part of your own share.

That instinct rarely holds up under real scrutiny. The founder economy isn't nearly as zero-sum as it can feel in the moment, and helping someone genuinely doesn't cost the person helping much of anything real, most of the time.

The founders who build the strongest reputations and the most valuable networks over time help generously and consistently, having actually worked through that scarcity instinct rather than being quietly ruled by it without ever examining it.
