TOPIC:
Founders rarely revisit whether their original target customer is still who they're actually serving

HOOK:
Who You Said You Were Building For at the Start Might Not Be Who You're Actually Serving Now

INTRO:
The original target customer gets set early and rarely re-examined. Point 2 is why that drift matters.

POINT 1:
A company's stated target customer, decided at founding, can quietly drift from who's actually buying and getting real value over time.

POINT 2:
Without noticing that drift, marketing and product decisions keep optimizing for a customer profile that's no longer accurate.

POINT 3:
The founders who stay sharp periodically check who's actually buying against who they originally said they were building for, and update accordingly.

CAPTION:
A company's stated target customer, decided early at founding, can quietly drift over time from who's actually buying, using, and getting real value from the product - not because the original thinking was wrong, but because markets and products both evolve.

Without noticing that drift explicitly, marketing messaging and product decisions keep optimizing for a customer profile that no longer accurately describes who the business is actually serving, creating a subtle but real mismatch.

The founders who stay sharp periodically check who's actually buying against who they originally said they were building for, and update their thinking - and their messaging - when the two have quietly diverged.
