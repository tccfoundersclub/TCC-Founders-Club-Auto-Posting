TOPIC:
Founders often underestimate how much a clear escalation ladder reduces anxiety on a team

HOOK:
Not Knowing Who to Go to for What Creates More Team Anxiety Than People Realize

INTRO:
Founders sometimes leave the escalation path informal. Point 2 is the quiet cost of that ambiguity.

POINT 1:
Without a clear sense of who to go to for what kind of problem, team members carry low-grade uncertainty about whether they're handling things right.

POINT 2:
That uncertainty shows up as hesitation, over-checking, or under-escalating - all of which slow things down and add stress.

POINT 3:
The founders who reduce team anxiety build a simple, explicit escalation structure, even a rough one, instead of leaving it to be figured out ad hoc.

CAPTION:
Without a clear, explicit sense of who to go to for which kind of problem, team members carry a quiet, low-grade uncertainty about whether they're handling things the right way - is this something I should just decide, or should I check with someone first.

That uncertainty doesn't stay invisible. It shows up as hesitation on decisions that should move fast, as over-checking on things that didn't need it, or as under-escalating something that genuinely needed attention sooner - all of it adding friction and stress that a clear structure would have prevented.

The founders who reduce this kind of team anxiety build a simple, explicit escalation structure, even a rough first version, instead of leaving people to figure out the right path ad hoc, case by case.
