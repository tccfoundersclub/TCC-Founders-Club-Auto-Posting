TOPIC:
Founders sometimes avoid telling a candidate the honest hard parts of the job during recruiting

HOOK:
Not Telling a Candidate the Hard Parts of the Job Doesn't Make Them Disappear. It Just Delays When They Find Out.

INTRO:
Founders sometimes soften the pitch to close a strong candidate. Point 2 is why that softening backfires.

POINT 1:
Underselling the genuinely hard parts of a role to make an offer more attractive works, until the new hire actually experiences those hard parts firsthand.

POINT 2:
A new hire who discovers the real difficulty after joining, without having been warned, trusts the founder less at exactly the moment trust matters most.

POINT 3:
The founders who retain people well tell candidates the honest, hard truth upfront, and let people opt in with full information instead of a filtered pitch.

CAPTION:
Softening or leaving out the genuinely hard parts of a role - the unpredictable hours, the ambiguity, the resource constraints - can make an offer land more easily with a strong candidate. It works right up until that new hire actually experiences those hard parts firsthand, unprepared.

Discovering the real difficulty of a role after joining, without ever having been warned about it, damages trust in the founder at exactly the moment - the first hard month - when that trust matters most for whether the person stays and pushes through.

The founders who retain people well tell candidates the honest, hard truth upfront, during recruiting, and let people opt in with full information instead of a carefully filtered version of the pitch.
