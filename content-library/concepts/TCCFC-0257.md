TOPIC:
Founders sometimes treat their own comfort with a decision as proof it's the right one

HOOK:
Feeling Comfortable With a Decision Isn't the Same as the Decision Being Right

INTRO:
Founders use their own comfort level as a signal. Point 2 is why that signal can mislead.

POINT 1:
A decision that feels comfortable is often just familiar, not necessarily correct - comfort and correctness aren't the same measurement.

POINT 2:
The right call is sometimes genuinely uncomfortable precisely because it requires real change, and comfort with the status quo can masquerade as confidence in it.

POINT 3:
The founders who make sharper decisions notice when comfort is doing the deciding, and check the actual reasoning separately from how the choice feels.

CAPTION:
A decision that feels comfortable to make is often just familiar - the option closest to what's already being done, the one that requires the least change - and familiarity isn't the same measurement as correctness, even though the two can feel identical in the moment.

The genuinely right call is sometimes uncomfortable precisely because it requires real change, and comfort with maintaining the status quo can masquerade as confidence in it, when it's really just an aversion to disruption.

The founders who make sharper decisions notice when comfort, rather than actual reasoning, is doing the deciding, and deliberately check the logic separately from how a given choice happens to feel.
