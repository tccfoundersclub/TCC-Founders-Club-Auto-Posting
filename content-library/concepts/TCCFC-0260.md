Revenue targets, growth rates, exit scenarios - all get defined clearly and specifically for the company. What "success" actually means to the founder personally, separate from any of those company metrics, often never gets the same clear definition.

Without that personal definition, it's genuinely possible to hit every company target and still feel a strange, unexpected emptiness - because the metrics, however impressive, were never actually the real goal, just proxies for something the founder never fully articulated.

The founders who sustain genuine fulfillment through the long haul take real time to define what success personally means to them, separate from the company's own scoreboard, and check their choices against that definition too.
