TOPIC:
Founders sometimes treat a co-founder's quietness as agreement rather than checking in

HOOK:
Your Co-Founder Going Quiet Isn't the Same as Your Co-Founder Being Fine

INTRO:
Founders read a quiet co-founder as low-maintenance. Point 2 is why that read can be dangerously wrong.

POINT 1:
A co-founder who's become quieter than usual can genuinely be fine, focused, and just heads-down - or quietly checked out and struggling.

POINT 2:
Those two states look identical from the outside, and only one of them means everything is actually okay.

POINT 3:
The founders who protect the partnership check in directly when something feels different, instead of assuming quiet means content.

CAPTION:
A co-founder who's grown quieter lately - less banter, shorter updates, less initiative in shared conversations - can genuinely just be heads-down and focused on their part of the business. They can also be quietly struggling, disengaging, or sitting on something they haven't found the words for yet.

Those two states look identical from the outside: quiet. There's no way to tell which one you're actually looking at without directly checking in, and it's easy to default to the more comfortable explanation simply because it requires nothing further.

The founders who protect the partnership check in directly the moment something feels different, rather than assuming quiet automatically means content.
