TOPIC:
Founders sometimes assume a customer who upgraded is more satisfied than one who didn't

HOOK:
A Customer Upgrading to a Higher Tier Doesn't Automatically Mean They're More Satisfied

INTRO:
Upgrades get read as a satisfaction signal. Point 2 is why that read can be incomplete.

POINT 1:
An upgrade can reflect growing satisfaction and real value, and it can also just reflect a need that forced the decision regardless of how the customer actually feels.

POINT 2:
Treating every upgrade as pure validation misses cases where a customer upgraded reluctantly, out of necessity, while quietly growing more frustrated.

POINT 3:
The founders who read customer health accurately look at satisfaction signals separately from tier or spend, instead of assuming they always move together.

CAPTION:
A customer moving up to a higher-priced tier feels like a clean signal of growing satisfaction - they like it enough to pay more. Sometimes that's exactly right. Sometimes the upgrade was forced by a need they had no real alternative for, and their actual satisfaction with the experience is flat or even declining underneath it.

Treating every upgrade as pure validation misses the cases where a customer upgraded reluctantly, out of necessity, while quietly growing more frustrated with parts of the experience that had nothing to do with the tier itself.

The founders who read customer health accurately track satisfaction signals separately from spend or tier level, instead of assuming the two always move together in the same direction.
