TOPIC:
Founders often don't realize how much their own vocabulary shapes the team's sense of urgency

HOOK:
The Word "Urgent" Loses Its Meaning Fast If You Use It for Everything

INTRO:
Founders reach for strong language to get attention. Point 2 is what overuse costs.

POINT 1:
Labeling routine requests as "urgent" or "critical" gets faster attention in the short term and dilutes what those words mean over time.

POINT 2:
Once everything is urgent, the team loses the ability to tell what's genuinely time-sensitive from what's just labeled that way out of habit.

POINT 3:
The founders who get fast responses when it truly matters are disciplined about reserving strong language for when it's actually warranted.

CAPTION:
Labeling a routine request as "urgent" or "critical" gets it moved to the top of someone's list faster - a natural, understandable shortcut when something feels important in the moment.

Used often enough, those words stop carrying real information. The team loses the ability to distinguish what's genuinely time-sensitive from what's simply been labeled that way out of habit, and the actual emergencies start competing for attention with everything else that's also been called urgent.

The founders who get genuinely fast responses when something truly matters are disciplined about reserving strong language for when it's actually warranted, protecting the word's meaning so it still works when it counts.
