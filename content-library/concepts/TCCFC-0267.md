TOPIC:
Founders sometimes wait for a formal review cycle to give feedback that would help sooner

HOOK:
The Feedback You're Saving for the Quarterly Review Would Probably Help More Right Now

INTRO:
Founders batch feedback into formal cycles. Point 2 is why that timing weakens it.

POINT 1:
Saving observed feedback for a scheduled quarterly review means it arrives months after the moment it was actually relevant.

POINT 2:
By the time formal feedback arrives, the specific context has faded, making it feel more like a verdict than a useful, actionable correction.

POINT 3:
The founders who help people improve fastest give feedback close to the moment it's observed, and use formal reviews for pattern-level, bigger-picture conversations instead.

CAPTION:
Noticing something worth addressing and saving it for the next scheduled review - weeks or months away - means it finally arrives long after the specific moment it was actually relevant to, disconnected from the context that made it useful in the first place.

By the time formal feedback arrives that late, the specific situation has faded from everyone's memory, and the feedback lands less like a useful, actionable correction and more like an unexpected verdict pulled from the past.

The founders who help people improve fastest give feedback close to the moment it's actually observed, and reserve the formal review for bigger-picture, pattern-level conversations that genuinely benefit from that longer view.
