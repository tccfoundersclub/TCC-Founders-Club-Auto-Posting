TOPIC:
Founders sometimes don't realize how much their own reaction to bad news trains what gets reported to them

HOOK:
How You React to the First Piece of Bad News Trains Your Team on Whether to Bring You the Second

INTRO:
Founders don't always notice the effect of their own reaction in the moment. Point 2 is why that first reaction matters so much.

POINT 1:
The way a founder reacts to the first piece of genuinely bad news - calm and constructive, or visibly upset and reactive - gets remembered by whoever delivered it.

POINT 2:
That memory directly shapes whether the same person brings the next piece of bad news quickly, or hesitates and softens it next time.

POINT 3:
The founders who stay well-informed are deliberately measured in the moment bad news lands, understanding that their reaction is training future behavior.

CAPTION:
The way a founder reacts to the first piece of genuinely bad news - staying calm and asking useful questions, or visibly reacting with frustration or panic - gets remembered clearly by whoever delivered it, often far more clearly than the founder remembers their own reaction.

That memory directly shapes what happens next time: whether the same person brings the next piece of bad news quickly and directly, or hesitates, softens it, or waits until it can't be avoided any longer.

The founders who stay genuinely well-informed are deliberately measured in the exact moment bad news lands, understanding - even in a hard moment - that their reaction is actively training how much and how fast they'll hear about the next problem.
