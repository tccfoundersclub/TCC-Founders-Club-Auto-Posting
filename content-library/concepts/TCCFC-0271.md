TOPIC:
Founders sometimes treat their most senior hire's departure as purely a loss, missing what it reveals

HOOK:
A Senior Person Leaving Isn't Just a Loss to Manage. It's Also Information Worth Examining.

INTRO:
Founders focus entirely on the logistics of a senior departure. Point 2 is the diagnostic opportunity that gets missed.

POINT 1:
A senior person leaving triggers real, practical concerns - the gap, the transition, the hiring search - that understandably take up most of a founder's attention.

POINT 2:
In that focus on logistics, the more uncomfortable question of what their departure might reveal about the organization often goes unexamined.

POINT 3:
The founders who learn the most from senior departures do an honest, private assessment of what the exit is actually telling them, not just how to fill the seat.

CAPTION:
A senior person leaving sets off a lot of real, practical concern - who covers the gap, how the transition gets handled, how fast a replacement search needs to move. All legitimate, and all of it can crowd out a harder, more uncomfortable question.

What does this departure actually reveal about the organization? Was it purely personal, or does it echo something other people have quietly felt too? That question often goes unexamined simply because the logistics are loud and immediate, and the diagnostic question is quieter and easier to skip.

The founders who learn the most from senior departures do an honest, private assessment of what the exit is actually telling them about the company, not just how to fill the seat that's now open.
