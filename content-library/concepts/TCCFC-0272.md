TOPIC:
Founders sometimes assume that being liked by investors means being trusted by them

HOOK:
Investors Liking You and Investors Trusting You Are Two Different Things

INTRO:
Founders read warm investor rapport as full confidence. Point 2 is why that read can be incomplete.

POINT 1:

POINT 2:

CAPTION:
An investor can genuinely enjoy a founder's company - warm rapport, easy conversation, real personal chemistry - and still hold real reservations about the business itself underneath that warmth. Liking someone and trusting their business plan are two separate evaluations, even though they can feel like one in a good meeting.

It's easy to read that personal warmth as full confidence and miss the more guarded signals sitting right alongside it - hesitant follow-up, vague next steps, questions that keep circling back to the same underlying concern.

The founders who read investor relationships accurately pay attention to actual behavior - follow-through, the specificity of diligence questions, how fast things move - rather than just how pleasant the conversation itself felt.
