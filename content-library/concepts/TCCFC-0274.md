TOPIC:
Founders sometimes forget to tell customers about improvements made based on their feedback

HOOK:
You Fixed the Thing a Customer Complained About. Did You Actually Tell Them?

INTRO:
Founders ship fixes based on feedback and move on. Point 2 is the follow-up step that often gets skipped.

POINT 1:
Shipping a fix based on customer feedback feels like the job is done, and the customer who originally raised it often never finds out it happened.

POINT 2:
That missed follow-up wastes an easy opportunity to show a customer their voice actually mattered and changed something real.

POINT 3:
The founders who build the strongest customer loyalty close the loop explicitly - "you mentioned this, we fixed it" - instead of assuming customers will notice on their own.

CAPTION:
A fix shipped in response to specific customer feedback feels like a completed task - problem raised, problem solved, move on to the next thing. The customer who originally raised it often never finds out it happened, unless they happen to stumble across the change themselves.

That missed follow-up wastes a genuinely easy, low-cost opportunity to show a customer their input actually mattered and changed something real - one of the strongest, cheapest loyalty-building moves available, left on the table by default.

The founders who build the strongest customer loyalty close the loop explicitly: "you mentioned this a few weeks ago, we fixed it, thank you for flagging it" - instead of assuming customers will simply notice on their own.
