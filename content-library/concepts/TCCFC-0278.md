TOPIC:
Founders sometimes wait for a formal exit interview to learn why someone actually left

HOOK:
By the Time the Exit Interview Happens, the Real Reason Someone Left Has Usually Already Softened

INTRO:
Exit interviews happen at the very end of the process. Point 2 is why that timing limits what they reveal.

POINT 1:
By the formal exit interview, someone's already mentally checked out and moving on - motivated more by a clean exit than a fully honest account.

POINT 2:
The real, harder reasons for leaving were often visible earlier, in smaller signals, if anyone had been paying attention or asking before the resignation.

POINT 3:
The founders who learn the most from departures pay attention to the signals before the resignation, not just the polished summary offered at the exit interview.

CAPTION:
By the time a formal exit interview happens, someone has usually already mentally checked out and is focused on a clean, uncomplicated exit rather than a fully honest account of everything that led them there - understandable, and it limits how much real signal that conversation actually carries.

The harder, more useful reasons for leaving were often visible earlier - in smaller signals, a shift in energy, an offhand comment - if anyone had been paying close enough attention or asking directly before the resignation was already final.

The founders who learn the most from departures pay attention to those earlier signals, treating the exit interview as a useful but late and partial source, not the primary place real understanding comes from.
