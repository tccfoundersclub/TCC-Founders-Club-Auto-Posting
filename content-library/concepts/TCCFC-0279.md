TOPIC:
Founders sometimes treat a big client logo as more important than the revenue it actually produces

HOOK:
A Recognizable Client Logo Feels Like a Big Deal. Check What It's Actually Worth in Revenue First.

INTRO:
A well-known client name carries real prestige. Point 2 is why that prestige can distort priorities.

POINT 1:
Landing a recognizable, well-known client name creates real excitement and social proof, independent of what that account actually contributes financially.

POINT 2:
Founders can end up over-investing time and resources in a prestigious-but-small account, chasing status rather than following the actual numbers.

POINT 3:
The founders who allocate resources well evaluate accounts by their real contribution, and treat prestige as a separate, secondary consideration.

CAPTION:
Landing a recognizable, well-known client name creates real excitement - something to put on the website, mention in a pitch, feel genuinely proud of. That excitement is completely independent of what the account actually contributes financially, which can be smaller than the prestige suggests.

It's easy to end up over-investing real time and resources chasing or maintaining a prestigious-but-small account, following status rather than following the actual numbers on where the revenue and growth are really coming from.

The founders who allocate resources well evaluate accounts primarily by their real contribution, and treat prestige as a genuinely separate, secondary consideration - nice to have, not a reason to skew priorities.
