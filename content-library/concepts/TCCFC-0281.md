TOPIC:
Founders sometimes assume a customer's public praise reflects their full private experience

HOOK:
A Glowing Public Review Doesn't Mean That Customer Doesn't Also Have Real Frustrations

INTRO:
Public praise gets taken as the complete picture. Point 2 is why it's often only part of the story.

POINT 1:
A customer can genuinely mean a glowing public review and still be sitting on real, unaddressed frustrations they never mentioned publicly.

POINT 2:
Public praise and private satisfaction aren't the same measurement - people are often more generous in public than they are candid in private.

POINT 3:
The founders who get the full picture ask happy-looking customers directly what's still frustrating, instead of assuming public praise is the whole story.

CAPTION:
A customer leaving a genuinely glowing public review means exactly what it says, and it's rarely the complete picture. The same customer can be sitting on real, unaddressed frustrations they simply never brought up publicly - a public review isn't the place most people air their full list of complaints.

Public praise and full private satisfaction are two different measurements. People are often more generous, and less specific, in a public review than they'd be in a private, direct conversation about what's actually working and what isn't.

The founders who get the full picture ask their happiest-looking customers directly what's still frustrating, instead of assuming a great public review means there's nothing left to improve.
