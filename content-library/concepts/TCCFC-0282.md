TOPIC:
Founders sometimes assume a mentor's silence on a plan means quiet approval

HOOK:
A Mentor Who Didn't Object to Your Plan Isn't the Same as a Mentor Who Endorsed It

INTRO:
Founders read a mentor's lack of pushback as agreement. Point 2 is why that read can be wrong.

POINT 1:
A mentor staying quiet during a plan walkthrough can mean genuine agreement, or simply not wanting to be the one to raise a hard question in the moment.

POINT 2:
Founders sometimes treat that silence as a green light, without ever directly asking what the mentor actually thinks.

POINT 3:
The founders who get real value from mentors ask directly for a genuine opinion, instead of inferring approval from an absence of objection.

CAPTION:
A mentor listening to a plan and not objecting can genuinely mean they think it's solid. It can also mean they had reservations and didn't feel it was their place to raise them uninvited, or didn't want to dampen the founder's visible enthusiasm in the moment.

Reading that silence as a green light skips the step that would have actually surfaced the real opinion - a direct question, asked plainly, instead of an inference drawn from what wasn't said.

The founders who get genuine value from mentors ask directly for an honest opinion - "what would you actually push back on here" - instead of quietly assuming approval from an absence of pushback.

DM "TRIBE" to practice asking mentors for real opinions, not just a reaction.
