Founders sometimes assume a team member's competence means they don't need check-ins

Your Most Competent Team Member Still Needs to Hear From You, Just Differently

INTRO:
High performers often get left alone. Point 2 is why that hands-off approach can backfire quietly.

POINT 1:
A highly competent team member who rarely needs help can end up with the least founder attention, simply because they're not generating visible problems.

POINT 2:

POINT 3:

CAPTION:
A highly competent team member who consistently delivers without drama can end up with the least attention from the founder, simply because they're not generating any visible problems that demand a check-in. It feels efficient - focus where the fires are.

Left unchecked for long enough, even genuinely strong, self-sufficient performers can quietly start to disengage, wondering privately whether their work is actually noticed at all, since it never seems to come up unless something's wrong elsewhere.

The founders who retain their best people check in deliberately with the ones who seem completely fine, not just the ones visibly struggling - a different kind of attention, but attention nonetheless.
