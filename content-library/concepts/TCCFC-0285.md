TOPIC:
Founders sometimes conflate a customer asking a lot of questions with a customer who's hard to work with

HOOK:
A Customer Asking a Lot of Questions Isn't Automatically a Difficult Customer

INTRO:
Founders sometimes dread the detail-oriented customer. Point 2 is why that instinct misreads the signal.

POINT 1:
A customer asking detailed, thorough questions is often simply being diligent, not difficult - a distinction that gets blurred under time pressure.

POINT 2:
Treating thoroughness as difficulty can lead to short, defensive answers that actually create the friction the founder was trying to avoid.

POINT 3:
The founders who build the best customer relationships welcome detailed questions as engagement, and answer them as generously as time allows.

CAPTION:
A customer who asks a lot of detailed, specific questions before or during a purchase can start to feel like extra work, especially under time pressure - easy to quietly label them as "difficult" simply because they're thorough.

That thoroughness is very often just genuine diligence - someone taking the decision seriously, wanting to actually understand what they're buying. Treating it as difficulty can lead to short, slightly defensive answers that create exactly the friction the founder was trying to avoid.

The founders who build the best customer relationships welcome detailed questions as a sign of real engagement, and answer them as generously and patiently as time allows, rather than treating curiosity as a burden.
