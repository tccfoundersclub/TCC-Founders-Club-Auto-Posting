TOPIC:
Founders sometimes assume that because a decision worked once, the same approach will work again

HOOK:
The Approach That Worked Perfectly Last Time Isn't Guaranteed to Work This Time

INTRO:
Past success creates a template founders reach for automatically. Point 2 is why blind repetition can fail.

POINT 1:
A decision-making approach that produced a great outcome once becomes a template a founder reaches for automatically in similar-looking situations.

POINT 2:
Circumstances change enough between decisions that the same approach, applied without re-examining the current context, can produce a very different result.

POINT 3:
The founders who make consistently good decisions treat past success as useful input, not a formula to apply without re-checking whether the situation still matches.

CAPTION:
A decision-making approach that worked out well once - a specific negotiation tactic, a particular hiring process, a way of handling a tough customer - naturally becomes a template a founder reaches for again the next time something similar comes up.

The trouble is that circumstances rarely repeat exactly. Applied without re-examining whether the current situation still actually matches the one that worked before, the same approach can produce a meaningfully different, sometimes worse, result.

The founders who make consistently good decisions treat past success as useful input worth considering, not a formula to apply automatically, and check each time whether the current context genuinely still fits.
