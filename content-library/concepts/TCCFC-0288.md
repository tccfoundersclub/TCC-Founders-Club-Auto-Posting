TOPIC:
Founders sometimes underestimate how much a rushed hire costs relative to a slower, more careful one

HOOK:
The Hire You Rushed to Fill a Gap Fast Usually Costs More Than the Gap Did

INTRO:
Urgency around an open role pushes founders to hire fast. Point 2 is the hidden cost of that speed.

POINT 1:
An open role creates real, visible pressure - work not getting done, a team stretched thin - that pushes toward filling it as quickly as possible.

POINT 2:
A rushed hire made under that pressure is statistically more likely to be a poor fit, and a poor fit costs far more in time and disruption than the original gap did.

POINT 3:
The founders who hire well protect a minimum bar of process even under real time pressure, understanding that a slower right hire beats a fast wrong one.

CAPTION:
An open role creates real, visible pressure - work piling up, a stretched team, a founder covering gaps themselves - all of it pushing hard toward filling the seat as fast as possible.

A hire made under that kind of rushed pressure is statistically more likely to turn out to be a poor fit, and a poor fit costs far more in wasted time, team disruption, and eventual re-hiring than the original open role ever did on its own.

The founders who hire well protect at least a minimum bar of real process even under genuine time pressure, understanding clearly that a slower right hire beats a fast wrong one nearly every time.
