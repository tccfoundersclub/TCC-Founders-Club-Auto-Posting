TOPIC:
Founders sometimes assume their own excitement about a feature predicts customer excitement

HOOK:
Just Because You're Excited About a Feature Doesn't Mean Your Customers Will Be

INTRO:
Founder enthusiasm and customer interest don't automatically align. Point 2 is why that gap catches founders off guard.

POINT 1:
A founder's personal excitement about a new feature is shaped by everything they know about the product's future direction - context customers don't share.

POINT 2:
Customers evaluate a feature purely by what it does for them right now, with none of the founder's broader excitement about where it fits strategically.

POINT 3:
The founders who ship features that land validate customer interest directly, instead of assuming their own enthusiasm is a reliable predictor of customer reaction.

CAPTION:
A founder's personal excitement about a new feature is shaped by everything they know - where it fits in the broader roadmap, what it unlocks strategically, how long the team worked to build it. None of that context is shared by the customer encountering it for the first time.

Customers evaluate a new feature purely by what it does for them right now, in their actual workflow, with zero access to the founder's excitement about where it fits into some larger strategic picture.

The founders who consistently ship features that land validate customer interest directly - real conversations, real testing - instead of assuming their own genuine enthusiasm is a reliable predictor of how customers will actually react.
