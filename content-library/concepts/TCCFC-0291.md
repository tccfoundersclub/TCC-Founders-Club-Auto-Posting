Founders sometimes assume a customer who negotiates hard on price will be a difficult customer overall

HOOK:
A Customer Who Negotiates Hard on Price Isn't Necessarily Going to Be a Difficult Customer Overall

INTRO:
Founders sometimes brace for ongoing difficulty after a tough negotiation. Point 2 is why that assumption doesn't always hold.

POINT 1:
A customer who negotiates firmly on price is often just being a careful, rational buyer - a completely separate trait from how they'll behave as an actual customer.

POINT 2:
Founders who read hard negotiation as a red flag for the whole relationship sometimes sour their own tone going into the partnership unnecessarily.

POINT 3:
The founders who build good relationships with price-sensitive customers treat the negotiation as its own contained conversation, not a preview of everything to come.

CAPTION:
A customer who pushes hard on price during negotiation can trigger a quiet assumption that they'll be difficult to work with overall - demanding, hard to please, a headache waiting to happen once the deal is signed.

Negotiating firmly on price is very often just careful, rational buying behavior, completely separate from how that same person will behave as an actual customer day to day - two different traits that get conflated under the stress of a tough negotiation.

The founders who build genuinely good relationships with price-sensitive customers treat the negotiation itself as its own contained conversation, and go into the actual working relationship with a clean slate rather than a preloaded assumption about what's coming.
