TOPIC:
Founders sometimes wait for a customer to explicitly complain before addressing a known limitation

HOOK:
You Already Know the Limitation. Waiting for a Customer to Complain About It First Doesn't Make It Less Real.

INTRO:
Founders sometimes deprioritize a known issue until someone actually raises it. Point 2 is why that timing backfires.

POINT 1:
A limitation the team already knows about internally doesn't become more or less real depending on whether a customer has complained about it yet.

POINT 2:
Waiting for the complaint means the issue is only ever addressed reactively, always a step behind, after it's already cost someone a bad experience.

POINT 3:
The founders who fix things proactively address known limitations on their own internal timeline, instead of waiting for external pressure to make it a priority.

CAPTION:
A limitation the team already knows about internally - a clunky flow, a missing feature, an edge case that breaks - doesn't become more or less real depending on whether a customer has gotten around to complaining about it yet. It's already true. The complaint is just when it becomes visible.

Waiting for that complaint before addressing it means the issue only ever gets fixed reactively, always a step behind, after it's already cost at least one customer a genuinely bad experience that a proactive fix would have prevented.

The founders who fix things proactively address known limitations on their own internal timeline, based on their own judgment of what matters, instead of waiting for external pressure to force the priority.
