Your Busiest Week and Your Most Productive Week Are Not Automatically the Same Week

INTRO:
Founders equate a packed, exhausting week with real progress. Point 2 is why that equation is often wrong.

POINT 1:
A week full of meetings, fires, and constant activity feels productive by sheer volume of motion, regardless of what actually got accomplished.

POINT 2:
The quieter weeks, with more space for focused work, often produce more real progress than the busiest, most chaotic ones.

POINT 3:
The founders who track real productivity look at outcomes achieved, not hours logged or how depleted the week felt by Friday.

CAPTION:
A week packed with back-to-back meetings, constant fires, and nonstop activity feels productive simply by the sheer volume of motion involved - by Friday, there's a real sense of having done a lot, even without a clear list of what actually got accomplished.

Quieter weeks, with more open space for genuinely focused work, often produce more real progress than the busiest, most chaotic ones - the busyness itself can crowd out the deep work that actually moves things forward.

The founders who track real productivity accurately look at outcomes actually achieved, not hours logged or how exhausted the week felt by the end of it - two very different measurements that get conflated easily.
