TOPIC:
Founders sometimes assume that because they built something, they're the best person to explain it to customers

HOOK:
Building the Product and Explaining It Clearly to a Customer Are Two Completely Different Skills

INTRO:
Founders assume deep product knowledge makes them the best explainer. Point 2 is why that assumption doesn't always hold.

POINT 1:
Deep familiarity with a product can make it harder, not easier, to explain simply - too much internal context gets assumed as obvious.

POINT 2:
A founder explaining their own product sometimes over-explains the parts that matter to them and under-explains the parts a new customer actually needs.

POINT 3:
The founders who communicate most clearly test their own explanations on people with zero context, instead of assuming deep expertise guarantees clarity.

CAPTION:
Deep familiarity with a product can genuinely make it harder to explain simply, not easier - so much internal context has become assumed and automatic that it's easy to skip explaining things a newcomer would actually need spelled out.

A founder explaining their own product sometimes over-explains the parts that feel most interesting or important to them personally, while under-explaining the more basic things a new customer actually needs answered first to understand what they're even looking at.

The founders who communicate most clearly test their own explanations directly on people with genuinely zero context, rather than assuming deep expertise about the product automatically guarantees clarity in explaining it.
