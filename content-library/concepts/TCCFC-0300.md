TOPIC:
Founders sometimes assume a customer's satisfaction survey score reflects their renewal likelihood

HOOK:
A High Satisfaction Score Doesn't Always Predict Whether a Customer Will Actually Renew

INTRO:
Founders treat survey scores as a direct proxy for retention. Point 2 is why the link is looser than it seems.

POINT 1:
A customer can rate their satisfaction highly on a survey and still churn, if the specific value they need shifts or a budget cut forces the decision.

POINT 2:
Satisfaction measures how someone feels about the experience so far, not whether the business case for continuing still holds.

POINT 3:
The founders who predict renewal accurately track usage depth and business alignment alongside satisfaction scores, not satisfaction alone.

CAPTION:
A customer rating their satisfaction highly on a survey is genuinely good news, and it doesn't automatically predict they'll renew - satisfaction measures how someone feels about the experience so far, which is a different question from whether the business case for continuing still holds for them.

A happy customer can still churn if their internal priorities shift, if a budget cut forces hard choices, or if the specific value they originally needed has changed - none of which shows up in a satisfaction score taken at one point in time.

The founders who predict renewal accurately track usage depth and business alignment alongside satisfaction, understanding that feeling good about a product and needing to keep paying for it aren't quite the same thing.
