TOPIC:
Founders sometimes assume that once a process is documented, the team will actually follow it

HOOK:
Writing Down a Process Doesn't Mean Anyone's Actually Going to Follow It

INTRO:
Documentation feels like the finish line. Point 2 is why adoption still needs separate work.

POINT 1:
A well-written process document solves the problem of information not existing - it doesn't solve the separate problem of people actually using it.

POINT 2:
Without reinforcement, a new process quietly gets ignored in favor of old habits, even when everyone agrees the new version is better on paper.

POINT 3:
The founders who get real process adoption treat documentation as step one, and follow it with active reinforcement until the new habit actually sticks.

CAPTION:
Writing a clear, well-structured process document solves a real problem: the information now exists somewhere, instead of living only in someone's head. It's a completely separate problem, and often the harder one, whether people actually use it day to day.

Without active reinforcement, a new process quietly gets ignored in favor of old habits, even when everyone in the room agreed at the time that the new version was clearly better - agreement in a meeting doesn't automatically change behavior back at the desk.

The founders who get real process adoption treat documentation as step one of a longer job, and follow it with genuine reinforcement - check-ins, reminders, visible modeling - until the new habit actually sticks.
