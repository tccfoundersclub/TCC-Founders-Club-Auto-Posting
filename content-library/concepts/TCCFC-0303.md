TOPIC:
Founders sometimes assume that hiring for a role means the role is now permanently solved

HOOK:
Filling a Role Doesn't Mean the Underlying Need Is Permanently Solved

INTRO:
Founders relax once a critical seat is filled. Point 2 is why that relief can be premature.

POINT 1:
Filling an important open role feels like closing a chapter - the gap is covered, attention can move elsewhere.

POINT 2:
The underlying need that created the role in the first place can keep evolving, meaning the hire that was right at signing may need to grow or the role may need to change.

POINT 3:
The founders who build durable teams keep checking whether a filled role still matches the company's evolving actual need, instead of treating "hired" as permanently solved.

CAPTION:
Filling an important open role feels like closing a chapter - real relief, the gap is finally covered, attention can shift elsewhere. That relief can be premature if the underlying need that created the role in the first place keeps evolving after the hire.

The person who was exactly right for the role at the moment of hiring may need real support to grow into where the role goes next, or the role itself may need to be reshaped entirely as the company's actual needs shift - neither of which "hired" as a status accounts for.

The founders who build durable teams keep periodically checking whether a filled role still genuinely matches the company's current, evolving need, instead of treating "we hired for that" as a permanently solved problem.
