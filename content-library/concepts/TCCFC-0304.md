TOPIC:
Founders sometimes assume their competitors are more coordinated internally than they actually are

You're Probably Assuming Your Competitor Has Their Act Together More Than They Actually Do

INTRO:
Founders see a competitor's polished external output and assume equally polished internals. Point 2 is why that assumption is usually generous.

POINT 1:
A competitor's clean external output - marketing, product releases, a confident public presence - suggests, wrongly, that everything behind the scenes is just as coordinated.

POINT 2:

POINT 3:

CAPTION:
A competitor's clean external output - sharp marketing, confident launches, a polished public presence - creates a natural assumption that everything happening behind the scenes is just as coordinated and buttoned-up. It almost never actually is.

Every company, without exception, has real internal chaos that never becomes visible externally - the same kind of messy meetings, unclear priorities, and half-finished projects any founder would recognize from their own operation, competitors absolutely included.

The founders who avoid unnecessary competitor-induced anxiety remember consistently that their own view of any competitor is always the polished surface, never the actual messy reality running underneath it, the same way their own competitors only see their polish too.
