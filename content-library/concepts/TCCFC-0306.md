TOPIC:
Founders sometimes assume a growing waitlist is proof of strong demand

A Growing Waitlist Feels Like Proof of Demand. It's Only Proof of Curiosity Until People Actually Pay.

INTRO:
Waitlist numbers get treated as validated demand. Point 2 is why that leap is premature.

POINT 1:

CAPTION:
Signing up for a waitlist costs almost nothing - a name, an email, thirty seconds of mild curiosity - which makes it one of the weakest possible signals of how many of those people will actually convert into paying customers once the real launch and the real price show up.

A large, fast-growing waitlist can create real, genuine founder confidence that doesn't always survive contact with an actual launch, where curiosity meets a real decision to spend money, and the numbers can look meaningfully different than the waitlist suggested.

The founders who validate demand accurately treat a waitlist as early curiosity worth noting, and look for a real, harder commitment - even something as small as a deposit or a pre-order - before calling the demand genuinely proven.
